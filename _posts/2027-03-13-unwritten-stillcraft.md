I have started more novels than I'd like to admit, and finished none. The part I always enjoyed was the beginning: the title, the first line, the blurb I imagined on the back. The other three hundred pages were the problem.

So I made an app where those three lines are the whole book. Unwritten is a cosy bookshop that sells books nobody ever wrote. Every day a customer comes to the counter with a handwritten note asking for one, and you find it on the shelf, which is to say you write it. Free, no ads, no account.

## What's inside

### A customer a day

The notes come from a catalogue of 420, in Russian and English:

- "A novel whose hero is a forgotten umbrella."
- "A phrasebook of Cat. My cat doesn't understand me."
- "The local train timetable to my childhood dacha. When does the last one leave?"

Some come with a wish: the whole book in a first line of six words, only its table of contents, no letter «e» anywhere, or only how it ends. If today's note doesn't speak to you, the brass bell on the counter calls another customer, up to three times a day. There are no streaks: skip a day and tomorrow someone else is waiting.

### Three lines are a book

Finding the book goes in short steps, each on one screen: what kind of book it is, its title, the first line and the blurb. That's enough to put it on the shelf. If you want more, there's a pen name, an epigraph, the contents, a page from the middle, the last line, the number of pages (it sets the width of the spine) and «see also», pointing at another book in the shop.

{% include figure.html path="blog/unwritten-post/shot-1.jpg" caption="What kind of book, and its title." %}

### Covers drawn in code

Every book gets a cover in one of four styles:

- a framed classic series with bookcloth and a little device;
- a three-band paperback;
- pulp with a halftone and a price tag;
- typed samizdat with staples.

Your own imprint goes on every one; you name it on the first evening.

{% include figure.html path="blog/unwritten-post/shot-2.jpg" caption="Found for an old lady in a wet coat." %}

### Inside the book

You turn its pages one at a time: the epigraph, a drop cap, the contents with dot leaders, a page from the middle. On the flyleaf you can leave a pencil note in the margin, as a reader. The library card at the back gets a violet stamp for every day you open the book.

{% include figure.html path="blog/unwritten-post/shot-3.jpg" caption="A preface to the second edition." %}

### Critics, readers, editions

Critics drop by when you call them, one review a day per book: a grumpy columnist, a professor who disagrees, a reading circle that argued till midnight without reading it, the bookseller's cat. A couple of weeks later a quiet book with empty margins comes back from a reader, and you read it again as someone else's. After a week on the shelf a book can come out in a second edition, revised, with a preface and one more chapter. Books that didn't work out aren't deleted: they go into the bargain box by the door at ten kopecks.

{% include figure.html path="blog/unwritten-post/shot-4.jpg" caption="The library card and the critics." %}

### Print it as a zine

Any book prints as an eight-page zine on one A4 sheet: fold it, make one cut, no glue. You can give someone a book that doesn't exist.

### The window

The shop has a window onto the street, and the sky in it is the real one: the time of day, the sun where it is now, the moon in tonight's phase, lit windows across the street in the evening. Pick a town in the settings and the rain, snow or fog is real too.

{% include figure.html path="blog/unwritten-post/shot-5.jpg" caption="Snow outside, a lamp inside." %}

## Good to know

**Do I have to write a whole book?**

No, and that's the point. Three lines make a book here.

**What leaves the phone?**

Only the town's coordinates, for the weather, and only if you picked a town. Every book stays on the phone.

## Get it

- **Requires:** Android 9.0 (API 28) or newer. No Google services needed.
- **Permissions:** internet, only for the weather outside the window.
- **License:** MIT

1. Download the APK on your phone.
1. Open it and allow installs from this source when Android asks.
1. Name your imprint, read today's note and find the book on the shelf.
