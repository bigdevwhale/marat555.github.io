My best thoughts come on the tram, in the shower and at two in the morning. By the evening most of them are gone, and the few I did write down sit in a notes app I never open again, between a shopping list and a Wi-Fi password.

Gossamer is my answer to both halves of that. Catching a thought takes two seconds. Then it sticks to a spider's web, and the web slowly shows which thoughts belong together. It's free, with no ads and no internet access at all.

## What's inside

### Two seconds, from anywhere

A widget on the home screen (write or speak), a tile in the quick settings, two shortcuts on the icon, «Share» from any app, and selected text. There is one field and four kinds of thought, each drawn as its own little thing caught in the silk: a firefly for an idea, a burr for a question, dandelion down for something noticed, a wisp of someone else's silk for a quote. No titles, no tags, no folders.

{% include figure.html path="blog/gossamer-post/shot-1.jpg" caption="One field, four kinds." %}

### Threads

Press a thought and drag a thread to another one. A thought with three threads or more is pulled in towards the hub, and its words are written under it. Nobody sorts anything: after a couple of weeks the middle of the web is simply what you keep thinking about.

If you write almost the same thing again, the spider ties it to the old one with a thicker thread. Thoughts that come back are usually the ones that matter.

{% include figure.html path="blog/gossamer-post/shot-2.jpg" caption="What matters ends up in the middle." %}

### Dew and wind

Every morning dew falls on three old thoughts, the ones you haven't opened for longest. Open one and it sparkles. The opposite happens too: a thought with no threads that nobody has looked at for a month starts to tremble, and then the wind takes it out of the window, into the Fluff. Nothing is deleted, and anything can be put back, but the web stays light without any inbox-zero rituals.

{% include figure.html path="blog/gossamer-post/shot-3.jpg" caption="Threads, and how long until the wind." %}

### The lure

Hang up an open question, like "should I go part-time?". In the evening, Gossamer offers to try the day's catch on it, one tap per thread.

### Cocoons and butterflies

When five to nine thoughts hang together, spin them into a cocoon and give it a name. It hangs from the window frame for three days, and you can't peek inside. Then you open it, reread everything at once and write one sentence: a decision, a plan, a first line, or an honest "not now". A butterfly flies out. Its wings are drawn from the cocoon's seed and coloured by the thoughts inside, one eye-spot for each.

{% include figure.html path="blog/gossamer-post/shot-4.jpg" caption="One sentence." %}

{% include figure.html path="blog/gossamer-post/shot-5.jpg" caption="What it all grew into." %}

### The window

The light changes with the hour, from dawn to a moonlit night, and with the season: frost in winter, a leaf caught in the web in autumn. In September gossamer drifts across the glass, and that's when you pick the year's twelve thoughts.

{% include figure.html path="blog/gossamer-post/shot-6.jpg" caption="Night thoughts glow." %}

## Good to know

**Where does my voice go?**

To the phone's own speech service, the same one the keyboard microphone uses. Gossamer itself has no internet permission.

**What if the wind takes something important?**

It's in the Fluff, one tap from coming back. You can also make the wind slower, or turn it off.

**Can I get my thoughts out?**

Yes: the whole web saves as Markdown to read anywhere, or as a backup file to restore on another phone.
