Every food app I've tried wants me to count something: calories, grams, glasses of water. I wanted the opposite, an app that only cares about one thing and makes it fun to chase. That thing is variety. The American Gut project found that people who ate more than thirty different plants a week had noticeably more varied gut flora than those who ate ten. Thirty sounds like a lot until you count the dill, the pinch of cumin and the handful of walnuts.

So I made a fairy tale. The Grey Gloom has drunk the colours of a kingdom, and every different plant on your plate gives back one of them. Dyer is free, with no ads, no account and no internet.

## What's inside

### One plate, one tap

You pick everything that was on the plate at once, and answer one question: how much of the plate was plants? A quarter, half or nearly all. That sets the brush, from thin to wide. Borscht, plov, a Greek salad and your own dishes are one tap each.

{% include figure.html path="blog/dyer-post/shot-1.jpg" caption="Everything on the plate, and one question." %}

### Seven paints

Each plant gives its pigment, and the paint spreads as watercolour over a grey ink drawing. Tomato paints the roof tiles, bilberries the evening sky, red cabbage the cathedral's rose window, onions light the windows, buckwheat and lentils lay the cobbles. Herbs and spices are drops worth a quarter; white rice and white bread are whitewash worth half. Kefir and sauerkraut work like a dyer's fixative: the week's colours come out fuller.

### The townsfolk

Everything that gets paint has someone who lives by it. The roofer is relieved his tiles aren't made of ash after all; the scarecrow is delighted to have its orange coat back; the water sprite in the castle moat likes it green. Each new plant also brings one real fact, like buckwheat being a cousin of rhubarb rather than a cereal.

### Thirty, and a festival

Thirty different plants in a week and the quarter throws a festival with garlands. Then the Gloom settles on the next quarter: the market, the mill, the harbour, the castle, in turn. In a hard week you can switch to the small brush of fifteen.

### Eight tries

A new taste usually takes eight to fifteen tries before you start to like it. So you can take a plant you dislike into work: its paint starts muddy and clears with every meal it's in, until on the eighth it's its own colour.

{% include figure.html path="blog/dyer-post/shot-3.jpg" caption="Beetroot, five tries of eight." %}

### A quiet meal

Put the phone face down and eat slowly. Fullness takes about twenty minutes to arrive, and every twenty quiet minutes the brush draws a small detail into the quarter: a cat in a window, geese by the pond, a fox by the road.

### The shelf, the guild and the dyer's book

Every plant you've ever eaten stands on the shelf as a jar of paint. The guild suggests three plants in season that the week is short of, with the easiest way to eat each. And the dyer's book opens real kitchen experiments as their plants appear: eggs dyed with onion skins, a red cabbage pH indicator, bilberry ink.

{% include figure.html path="blog/dyer-post/shot-2.jpg" caption="The shelf and the guild's order." %}

### The kingdom

Every week stays in an album. Once all four quarters have had a festival, you can paint the Gloom herself.

## Good to know

**Do I have to count anything?**

No. No calories, no grams, no portions. Sweets and crisps simply paint nothing; there's no punishment anywhere.

**Is thirty a rule?**

It's an observation from one large study, not a law, and the app isn't a doctor. It's a nice number to aim at.

**What leaves the phone?**

Nothing. The app has no internet permission at all.

## Get it

- **Requires:** Android 9.0 (API 28) or newer. No Google services needed.
- **Permissions:** notifications, only for the evening question.
- **License:** MIT.

1. Download the APK on your phone.

1. Log your next plate and watch the quarter come back to colour.
