Many of us grew up with pencil marks on a kitchen door frame. Those lines end up being some of the most precious things in a home, and they deserved an app.

A door frame for each child, the kind you mark their height on, with a notch for every week of their life that has a photo or a clip. Your phone's own camera does the shooting, with every mode it has; when you come back, the new pictures go on the door frame. A copy is kept in Notch, the originals stay in your gallery, and old pictures from the gallery land in the week they were taken. Free, no ads, no account, nothing leaves the phone.

## What's inside

### Smart reminders

Only when a week has no picture: midweek and on its last day, at the half hour your family usually shoots on that day of the week, learned from your own pictures. Never at night. "Masha is 2 years 3 months. This version of her won't come again."

### Tree rings

A childhood as the cross-section of a tree: a ring a year, 52 weeks round each, the notched weeks in the child's own crayon. Tap a ring for its year: pictures, weeks notched, the longest run, a height chart.

{% include figure.html path="blog/notch-post/shot-1.jpg" caption="Further down. Heights in pencil, a line on every birthday." %}

### The same spot

Mark a photo "repeat this spot": by the door, on the sofa, with the bear. Next time the earlier picture lies faintly over your phone's camera, so the child can be stood where they stood. A year ago is offered too.

### They grow in place

The pictures of one spot become a film: the phone finds the faces and lines them up, and each picture melts into the next with the age written beneath.

{% include figure.html path="blog/notch-post/shot-2.jpg" caption="Tree rings. A ring a year, 52 weeks round." %}

### A second a day

A short clip a day; the film takes one second from each, with the date and age in pencil in the corner. A month, or a year in six minutes.

### It will soon be gone

42 things of each age that leave without anyone noticing: holding your finger with the whole hand, starfish sleep, the mixed-up words, the gap-toothed smile. Each says how long it usually lasts. Keep it with a photo, a clip or their voice.

{% include figure.html path="blog/notch-post/shot-3.jpg" caption="Made from it. Films, hands, the same age." %}

### Their words

"Appo" for apple, in their own voice: hold to record. Whole sentences too: "Why does the moon follow our car?"

### Birthday interview

The same twelve questions every year, answered on video with the phone's camera. Play a year's answers one after another, or one question's answers across the years.

{% include figure.html path="blog/notch-post/shot-4.jpg" caption="Hands. Traced on A4, to scale." %}

### Hands

A hand on a sheet of A4, twice a year. Notch finds the sheet and the hand and traces round it to scale, and the outlines sit one inside the other. If it can't find them, draw round with a finger.

### At the same age

Brothers and sisters each get their own door frame. Put your son today next to your daughter when she was exactly as old.

{% include figure.html path="blog/notch-post/shot-5.jpg" caption="Birthday interview. Twelve questions, every year." %}

### The month's postcard

On the 1st, the month on one card: a few prints, the ages and heights, a word they said. One tap sends it to the family chat.

### Nothing leaves the phone

No account, no cloud, no analytics, no internet permission. Faces are lined up by Android's own face finder, on the phone. Every picture, clip and recording goes into one backup zip.

{% include figure.html path="blog/notch-post/shot-6.jpg" caption="The postcard. The month on one card." %}

## Good to know

**What counts as a notch?**

Any photo or clip of the child in a week of their life. One is enough. Weeks before you started with Notch are "before the door frame", not missed.

**Are the originals kept?**

Yes. The originals stay in your gallery; Notch keeps its own copy, so nothing breaks if you tidy the gallery later.

**Can I add pictures I took before?**

Yes. Pick them from the gallery and each one lands in the week it was taken, so the door frame fills in back to the day they were born.

**Does it need the internet?**

No. It doesn't even ask for the permission. No account, no cloud, no ads, no analytics; pictures go nowhere unless you share them.

**What if I change phones?**

Settings → Backup saves every picture, clip and recording into one zip. Restore it on the new phone.

## Get it

- **Requires:** Android 8.0 (API 26) or newer and a camera app. No Google services needed.
- **Permissions:** Photos and videos, to take in what your camera shoots and to import old pictures. The microphone for their words. Notifications for the reminders. Optionally, display over other apps for the ghost over the camera. No internet at all.
- **License:** MIT

1. Download the APK on your phone.
1. Open it and allow installs from this source when Android asks.
1. Add a child with a name, a birthday and a crayon, then tap Make a notch.
