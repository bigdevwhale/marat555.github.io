I like dynasty games: the moment your old king dies and you suddenly play his clumsy son is the best part of Crusader Kings. But those games are about thrones. Most families in history held a plough, not a crown, and their dynasties were just as long. I wanted that game in the size of a phone, told the way such families told their own stories: as a folk tale.

Seventh Generation starts with a curse. A beggar woman turned away without bread, a herbwoman on the pyre, a white fox killed at a shrine: someone curses your founder's family *to the seventh generation*. Carry the blood that far and the curse falls away. If the last of the line dies childless, the family ends. Free, no ads, no account, no internet.

## What's inside

### Three lands, three ways of drawing

- **Rus, 1690–1950**, drawn as a lubok folk print: an izba, a barin, a matchmaker, the recruit levy, 1812, emancipation, the collective farm.
- **Medieval Europe, 1180–1460**, an illuminated manuscript with gold leaf: seigneur and villein, the fairs of Champagne, crusades, the Black Death, the Jacquerie, the Maid of Orléans.
- **Japan, 1662–1950**, an ukiyo-e woodblock print: the daimyo's procession, the pilgrimage to Ise, the Black Ships, Meiji, the great Kanto earthquake.

Each has its own names, money, rival family and history, and every picture is drawn in code in that land's style.

### A bean from the bag

Every risky deed is settled by a bean drawn from a bag: white is luck, black is misfortune, red is the curse. The higher the stat a deed needs (strength, wits, heart, cunning, faith, health), the more white beans; traits, relics and the family legacy add more. While the curse holds, one red bean always waits in the bag. Each choice shows its chance before you draw.

### Blood and temper

Children are born with blood traits (a giant, frail, beautiful, quick-witted, long-lived, barren) and grow into tempers: kind or cruel, brave or cowardly, honest or lying. Acting against your temper lays a stone on the soul. Five stones, and something breaks: the tavern, a monastery, a long walk to a holy place.

### Things come back

A soldier you hid comes back years later as a bandit or a benefactor. Stolen grain surfaces in court. A foundling grows up. A debt to the neighbours comes due. A family can leave the plough for a trade, a craft, a guild or a temple, and then lives a different life. There are 75–84 everyday events and 18–22 historical ones in each land, so two games rarely look alike.

### Succession

When the head of the family dies, you play any living descendant. Pass over the eldest son and he bears a grudge. The dead leave a last wish; fulfil it for glory, or they come back in dreams. The whole bloodline grows into a tree of medallions, with the red thread of the cursed line running through it.

## Good to know

**How long is a game?**

A family reaches the seventh generation in roughly 150–200 years of game time, an evening or two. About four games in ten lift the curse when played at random; you can do better.

**Is it accurate?**

The history events are real and dated; the village life is written to be true to its time rather than to one particular village. It's a folk tale with real history at the window.
