Over the last months a lot of my apps grew pets. A puffin breathes with you in Puffling, a team trains in Pumpling's basement gym, a hamster keeps the books in Pennyling's burrow, a cat sits in Reelkin's projection booth. Each of them lives in its own app, and I kept wishing I could see them all at once. Lingvale is that place: a small pixel valley where every app gets a house, and the residents move in with everything they did.

It's free, with no ads and no account, and it doesn't even have internet access. It only reads what the other apps on the same phone show it. This is the first version.

## What's inside

### You build the houses

The valley starts empty: a path, a stream with a plank bridge, eight clearings and one more at the bottom. You choose which houses go up – the tea house for Puffling, the basement gym for Pumpling, the burrow for Pennyling, the cinema for Reelkin, the academy tower for Inkling, the tavern for Questling, the studio for Songling, the writing desk for Keyling, the reading room for Familiar. Only the apps on your phone can build a house, and nothing else clutters the map. The house's team walks the grass in front of it and talks about its day; at night the windows of the houses that were busy this week are lit.

### Fireflies

Everything you do in the apps brings the village fireflies: a practice, a workout, a record, a film, a lesson, a game, a song, a page. When two or three houses are busy on the same day there are more of them, and three busy houses make a fair with flags across the valley. The shop sells sixteen things for them, from a bench to a hot air balloon. Some do more than stand there: the notice board brings village news, the post box a letter about the week gone by, and with a campfire the residents gather by the fire in the evening. The more fireflies gathered, the bigger the village: a hamlet, a village, a large village, a township, a small town.

### A look inside

Tap a house and you're in the app's own room with its pets. Three tabs: the week at a glance (what was done, minutes, the streak, busy days of the month, each against the time before), the residents with how close each is to growing, and the history – twelve weeks, the month against the last one, the house's rhythm by time of day and its book, written by hand.

### The stamp album

Every creature of every house in one album, a page per house. The ones you've met are stamps franked with a postmark; the rest wait in empty mounts with their number.

{% include figure.html path="blog/lingvale-post/shot-2.jpg" caption="The album: 144 of 232 collected." %}

### Days

One day across all the apps, hour by hour, with a lane per house; tap a lane to see what happened. The week by house, which houses tend to be busy together, a village gazette for every day and a patchwork quilt of the year, a patch a day sewn from the fabrics of the busy houses.

## Good to know

**Do I have to enter anything?**

No. Lingvale reads everything from the apps themselves; you only choose which houses to build.

**How do the apps talk to it?**

Each app has a small read-only bridge. Lingvale asks it for the pets, the days and the last week's events, and the app answers only a Lingvale signed with my key. Nothing is changed in the apps and nothing leaves the phone.

**What if I take a house down?**

Nothing is lost: the app keeps all its data. Build the house again and the residents come back.

## Get it

- **Requires:** Android 9 (API 28) or newer, and at least one of Puffling, Pumpling, Pennyling, Reelkin, Inkling, Questling, Songling, Keyling or Familiar in its November 2026 version or newer.
- **Permissions:** none, not even internet. No account, no ads, no analytics.
- **License:** MIT. Fonts: PT Serif, Caveat, Nunito and Inter (OFL).

1. Update the apps whose pets you want in the village.
1. Download the APK on the phone, open it and allow installs from this source when Android asks.
1. Pick the first house to build.
