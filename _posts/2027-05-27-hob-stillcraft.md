Every house has a top shelf where things go to be forgotten. The winter boots the older child grew out of, waiting for the younger one. The box with the tree decorations, searched for every December. The extension lead, the warranty cards, the drill a friend borrowed in spring. You remember that you have it; you don't remember where.

In Russian folklore every house has a domovoi, a small furry spirit who lives behind the stove and keeps the household in order. In English he'd be a hob. Hob is an app with one living in it: write a thing down once, and he remembers where it lies. It's free, with no ads, no account and no internet permission. This is the first version.

## What's inside

### Where is it?

Ask the hob for "extension lead", "skates" or "fairy lights", and the answer is a path: pantry, shelving, box 3. He searches names, rooms, places, box labels and numbers, and you can browse by kind too: clothes, tools, papers, holidays, or kinds of your own.

Writing a thing down takes a name. The sign and the kind are guessed from it, so "winter jacket 116" turns into a coat in the clothes. A photo can be added, taken by the phone's own camera.

{% include figure.html path="blog/hob-post/shot-1.jpg" caption="Everything in the clothes kind, and who it fits." %}

### Your own house

Places, rooms and the spots in them are yours to name: the flat, the dacha, the garage; the top shelf, the wardrobe, under the bed. Boxes get numbers, and you write the number on the box with a marker, which is a lot faster than a label printer. A room with nothing written down stays a dark corner until you open its cupboards.

{% include figure.html path="blog/hob-post/shot-2.jpg" caption="The hallway: the top shelf with two boxes, the wardrobe with papers." %}

### The hob's notes

The home screen is where the hob leaves notes: the tree decorations will be needed soon; Serge still has the drill; the neighbour's step-ladder has lived here two months; a child has grown into things on the shelf. A thing can be marked with the month it's needed, a loan with the day to ask for it back, and the reminder goes through any messenger. Once a day, late in the morning, there's one notification, and only when there's news.

### Clothes to grow into

This is where the app started. With two children, the older one's clothes don't pass down straight away: they wait for years in the far corners of cupboards, and by the time they fit, nobody remembers they exist or what size they were.

Add the children's height and shoe size, and write the size from the label: "116", "110–116", "4–5 years", "30–33". The hob works out when each thing will fit: from the child's own pace once they've been measured twice, from the usual height for their age before that. A thing keeps its history, from the cousin who wore it first to whoever comes next, and when a child outgrows it, it goes back on a shelf to wait for the next one.

### Clear-out

Things nobody has touched for over a year come up one at a time: keep, give away, sell or throw out. Papers, seasonal things and clothes waiting for a child are left alone. In doubt, keep it; the hob will ask again next year. After a clear-out, as tradition demands, you leave him some milk.

{% include figure.html path="blog/hob-post/shot-5.jpg" caption="The old vacuum cleaner, untouched for three years." %}

### Why come back

The hob likes lit rooms, clean shelves, things that come back and fresh measurements, and together they make the house's cosiness. His title grows with it, from guest to lodger, master and old-timer, and there are signs for the first boxes, the first clear-out, a coat worn by two children. His tab also keeps what has gone on to other homes and what the sold things brought in.

The hob himself was drawn with ChatGPT from a description; the rest of the app is plain Compose.

## Good to know

**Can the whole family share one house?**

Not yet. For now the house lives on one phone; to move it to another, save a copy of the house in Settings and open it there. A house shared between phones is the next thing I want to build.

**How does it know when clothes will fit?**

From the size on the label and the child's measurements. Two measurements a few months apart give the child's own pace; with one, it uses the usual height for the age.

**It's the first version. What if something breaks?**

Please tell me: Settings has buttons to report a bug or suggest an idea through the StillCraft bot.

## Get it

- **Requires:** Android 9 (API 28) or newer.
- **Permissions:** notifications, for the morning reminder. Photos are taken by the phone's own camera, and there's no internet permission at all.
- **License:** MIT. Fonts: Lora and Nunito (OFL).

1. Download the APK on the phone.
1. Open it and allow installs from this source when Android asks.
1. Choose your rooms, then write down the first few things and where they lie.
