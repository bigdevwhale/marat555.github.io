Everyone who loves games has a pile. The Steam sale you couldn't resist, the board game a friend swore by, the RPG you were saving for a long holiday. Backlog trackers treat that pile as a debt: a percentage, a progress bar, a quiet reproach every time you open the app. I wanted the opposite. A room where the pile can just lie in its corner, and finishing a game feels like an event.

So I put a mimic in the room. Mimic is a pixel shelf for video games, board games and tabletop RPGs, with a chest on the rug that eats the games you finish. It's free, with no ads and no account.

## What's inside

### The room

Everything you own stands on wall shelves, each box drawn by what it came in: cartridges, disc cases, big board game boxes, key cards for digital copies. What you bought and never opened settles into a heap in the corner, and dust bunnies move onto anything nobody touched for half a year. Over the TV burns a wisp for every game in your hands; a crow on the sill means a box is away at a friend's. The window shows your town's real sky: sunrise and sunset at the right time, rain, snow, fog, a storm with lightning.

### Adding a game

Type a title and the mimic searches Wikidata and Wikipedia: the year, the maker, the players and the cover come in, and the cover is redrawn in pixels to sit with the room (there's a setting to show covers as they are). There are popular genres for each kind, 28 for video games, 21 for board games and 14 for tabletop RPGs, and you can make your own genres and subgenres. Or just snap the box with the phone's camera.

{% include figure.html path="blog/mimic-post/shot-1.jpg" caption="Every box, searchable." %}

### Three in hands

You can hold three games at once, no more. When you put one down, you write where you stopped: "Novigrad, Dandelion's Chameleon. Play gwent with the innkeeper." Months later, when you come back, the wisp reads the note to you first. Sessions keep how long you played, a few words, and screenshots from the gallery or a photo of the screen.

{% include figure.html path="blog/mimic-post/shot-2.jpg" caption="A game in hands: the note and the sessions." %}

### The credits

Finished? Write one line, pick what the game gave you (hooked me, rested, I'll dream of it), add stars and a short review, and hand it over. The chest opens wide, the box drops in, the mimic chews, and the box goes back to the shelf with a bite out of its corner. The genre leaves a trait on the chest: fangs from horror, a scroll in its teeth from RPGs, a straw hat from farming games, dice eyes from roguelikes, meeple teeth from euro games, a party hat, springs from platformers. After a few years no two mimics look alike.

### When you can't choose

"Mimic sniffs" asks three things: how much time you have, who's playing, and whether there's energy for your head, your hands or your heart. Then it pulls three boxes from the pile. If you still can't decide, a twenty-sided die picks one, and if it rolls a one, it's embarrassed and rolls again. The dig brings up the oldest box from the very bottom: give it half an hour, or let it go, and it drifts off as a little ghost. No hard feelings.

{% include figure.html path="blog/mimic-post/shot-3.jpg" caption="Three boxes from the pile." %}

### The chronicle and the bestiary

The chronicle counts the year: games and hours by month, what the mimic ate, your average rating and your game of the year, how long a box usually waits before it's opened. The bestiary collects everyone who has turned up in the room, and keeps the rest as silhouettes with a hint.

## Good to know

**Does it import my Steam library?**

Not yet. Steam now hides the list of games behind a login, so an import needs a bit more work. It's on the list.

**What leaves the phone?**

Only the catalogue search, when you search, and the weather for the window, which sends nothing but your town's coordinates. Games, notes, sessions and screenshots stay on the phone.

## Get it

- **Requires:** Android 9.0 (API 28) or newer. No Google services needed.
- **Permissions:** internet, for the catalogue search and the weather.
- **License:** MIT.

1. Download the APK on your phone.
1. Open it and allow installs from this source when Android asks.
1. Add the game you're playing now and the one you finished last.
