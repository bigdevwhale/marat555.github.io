Every list of "small things that make a day better" I've written ended the same way. I read it, I agreed with all of it, and I did none of it, because choosing a treat for yourself is a chore and there's no surprise in it. A present works because someone else picked it and you didn't see it coming. So I wanted the someone else to be me, a few weeks ago, and I wanted to forget what I'd picked.

Capsule is a capsule vending machine, the kind with a glass dome and a crank, that you fill with little presents for future days. A day after you put one in, your words are sealed. One coin a day, a full turn of the crank, and a capsule rolls into the tray with something past-you thought you'd like. Free, no ads, no account, no internet.

## What's inside

### Filling the machine

Write a present and pick its kind: a *small joy* (five free minutes, no money), a *thing* to buy, an *outing*, a rare *golden* one you save up for, or just a *note* to yourself. Tag when it would rather fall: morning, evening, Friday, the weekend, sun, rain, grey sky or snow. Only the colour of the shell tells the kind.

{% include figure.html path="blog/capsule-post/shot-3.jpg" caption="Fill the machine: five kinds, tags for when, and a hiding place." %}

### Forgetting is the feature

For a day you can still read your words and take them back. Then they're sealed, the capsule rolls in among the others, and your own capsules can't fall until that day is over. A few weeks later you won't remember what's in there, and that's the point.

### A coin, a crank, a lid

You get one coin a day, and the purse keeps up to three for days you miss. Tap the slot, turn the crank a full round clockwise (the ratchet clicks, the capsules jump) and one drops into the tray with a thud. Twist the lid sideways until it pops, and the paper unrolls with the present and a line about when you put it in and what the weather was like.

{% include figure.html path="blog/capsule-post/shot-1.jpg" caption="Twist the lid: drag sideways or just keep tapping." %}

### The machine reads the day

Look out of the window and mark the weather. Capsules tagged for today fall more often, your own more often than the factory's, and gold rarely, at most once a month. Things to buy keep to a monthly limit you set, so a good week doesn't empty your wallet.

### Hiding places

Buy a little something, hide it at home, and leave a riddle with a photo of the place, shot with your phone's own camera. Weeks later the riddle falls out of the machine. The photo opens only if you haven't found it in three days.

{% include figure.html path="blog/capsule-post/shot-2.jpg" caption="A hiding place falls out: the riddle first, the photo only later." %}

### Hard day

Capsules tagged "hard day" are kept back from the crank. A dark button under the machine gives you one of them, once a day, without a coin, for the days when you need it more than you need a surprise.

### It falls by itself

Now and then a capsule drops on its own at a random moment of the day, with one quiet notification. Not every day: about three days in ten.

### Figures and the album

Every capsule holds a little figure. Do the present, close it with one word about how it was, and the figure stands on the shelf: four series of eight (kitchen garden, forest, sea and sky), with gold ones from golden capsules. The papers go into an album by month.

{% include figure.html path="blog/capsule-post/shot-4.jpg" caption="The shelf and the album of done presents, one word each." %}

## Good to know

**What if I don't fill it?**

A factory batch of 165 ready-made capsules, in English and Russian, tops the dome up when it runs low, so there's a surprise even before you write your first one. You can switch it off and keep only your own.

**What if I don't want today's capsule?**

Two swaps a week put it back into the dome, where it gets lost among the rest. If a present really can't be done, you can let it go.

## Get it

- **Requires:** Android 9.0 (API 28) or newer. No Google services needed.
- **Permissions:** notifications only, for the capsule that sometimes falls by itself. Photos of hiding places are taken with your phone's own camera app, so Capsule never asks for camera access. No internet, no contacts, no account.
- **License:** MIT

1. Download the APK on your phone.
1. Open it and allow installs from this source when Android asks.
1. Put in your first present, forget it, and turn the crank tomorrow.
