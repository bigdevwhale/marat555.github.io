Every old flat has a story nobody told you when you moved in. A piano used to stand by that wall. Someone waited at that window every New Year's Eve. I wanted a ghost story that happens in your own home, slowly and quietly, with no jump-scare treadmill — and one that ends kindly, because the scariest thing about a ghost is that it is still waiting for something.

Lodger turns your flat into the scene. You photograph your rooms, and every evening someone leaves a mark in them: shoe prints across the hallway, a chair that wasn't there, a word written in breath on the mirror, a dark shape by the sofa that is only in the picture. Notes arrive in the evening, words come through the static of an old radio, clues pin themselves to a cork board. After two weeks the story comes together, and there is one real thing to do so the lodger can finally go. Free, no ads, no account, no internet.

## What's inside

### Your rooms, your photos

Photograph the hallway, kitchen, room, bathroom and balcony as they look in the evening, with the phone's own camera. Two rooms are enough. If your flat has no balcony, cross it out and its marks go next door. The photos never leave the phone.

### Marks painted into the picture

Every few nights something turns up: dusty prints with a tread, wet footprints, a cat's trail, crumbs laid out in a horseshoe, a handprint on the mirror, frost in October, eyes under the sofa, a shadow standing where nobody stands. The app reads each photo for its darkest corner, its glass and its stretch of floor, and paints the mark into the picture so it takes the room's own light. A new mark develops slowly when you open the room, like a print in the tray.

{% include figure.html path="blog/lodger-post/shot-1.jpg" caption="The flat on night ten: something has changed in one of the rooms." %}

### Notes in the evening

The lodger writes — grumpy, frightened, polite, seven years old. A note written at three in the morning still waits for the evening: the phone stays silent at night.

{% include figure.html path="blog/lodger-post/shot-2.jpg" caption="Notes from a music teacher who has been waiting since 1971." %}

### The radio

Switch it on and turn the dial through live static. Near a voice the hiss thins and letters come through; hold the dial and the word is caught, spoken low by the phone's own voice. A new word comes on air every couple of nights, and together they make one sentence.

{% include figure.html path="blog/lodger-post/shot-3.jpg" caption="Words caught so far: “he never finished…”" %}

### The cork board

Photographs, letters, tickets, records and objects pin themselves to the board one by one, tied together with red thread. When every clue is there and every word is caught, the story comes together.

{% include figure.html path="blog/lodger-post/shot-4.jpg" caption="Clues on the board, three of five." %}

### One thing left undone

Then comes a real task, done in the real world: play a nocturne at seven in the evening, write a letter by hand and post it, invite someone round, look at the sky for ten minutes, give a nameless cat a name. Do it, tap "Done", and the lodger goes. The flat stands empty for three nights, and then someone else moves in.

{% include figure.html path="blog/lodger-post/shot-5.jpg" caption="The story, and the unfinished business." %}

## Good to know

**How scary is it?**

You choose. Candle is quiet: marks, notes, the radio. Lantern adds the odd knock and photos that waver as if the lamp were failing. Full moon lets you glimpse, for a blink, whoever stands in the photo.

**Does it need the camera permission?**

No. Photos are taken by the phone's own camera app, or picked from the gallery.

## Get it

- **Requires:** Android 9.0 (API 28) or newer. No Google services needed.
- **Permissions:** notifications only, for the evening notes. No camera permission, no internet, no account.
- **License:** MIT

1. Download the APK on your phone.
1. Open it and allow installs from this source when Android asks.
1. Photograph two or more rooms in the evening, and let them move in.
