As a child I knew exactly what lived under the bed. It had yellow eyes, it waited for the light to go out, and it ate children who let a foot hang over the edge. I grew up, and the thing under the bed was replaced by deadlines, test results and the news.

So I decided to make friends with it. Underbed is a pet that lives under your bed and eats fear. You feed it the horror films you watched and the horror books you read. Anxious news counts too, but it's fast food. And your own fears it takes for the night and keeps watch over. Free, no ads, no account.

## What's inside

### Who lives there

There are seven to choose from: the Bogey, a Ghost, a Zombie, a Vampire, a Werewolf, a Doll and a Kikimora from Slavic folklore. All of them are drawn in cross-hatched ink, like an old Victorian picture book. The only colour is in their eyes and in the belly glow when they're fed. Each one has favourite kinds of horror: the Vampire loves gothic, the Kikimora loves folk horror. The Werewolf howls at the real full moon.

### The goosebump meter

You tell it what you watched or read. Films and books are found as you type: a bundled catalogue of about 300 horror classics works offline, and online the app asks Wikidata for films and Fantlab and Open Library for books. Then comes the goosebump meter. You hold a finger on the screen for as long as it was scary, bumps rise, and the level climbs from "a little" to "still scared". Last, you say where you felt it: the nape of the neck, the belly, the heels.

{% include figure.html path="blog/underbed-post/shot-1.jpg" caption="Feeding: hold a finger down while it's scary." %}

### News is fast food

This is the part I care about most. Made-up fear in a film or a book is safe, and it fills the pet up: it grows, and five of a kind make something sprout on it. Gothic gives it a top hat, folk horror twig antlers, monsters tentacles, J-horror long wet hair. News fills it up only a little. After three a day it covers its mouth with its paws and says: "I'm full. Go for a walk, I'll keep watch."

### The graveyard

Every film and every book it eats gets a grave on the hill. The stone matches the kind of horror:

- a cross with a raven for gothic;
- a roofed wooden post for folk horror;
- an obelisk with an eye for monsters;
- stacked stones for J-horror;
- an open book with a red bookmark for any book.

You can write an epitaph on any of them. The moon above the graveyard is the real moon in its real phase.

{% include figure.html path="blog/underbed-post/shot-2.jpg" caption="The graveyard on the hill." %}

### The night watch

Before sleep you name what frightens you tonight: the dentist tomorrow, a noise behind the wall, a talk with the boss. You turn the light out, and the screen becomes a dim night light with only two eyes under the bed. In the morning there's a report: "In the night came the dentist and the noise behind the wall. I ate them. It was the size of a wardrobe, now it's a pea."

### Film night

Put on a horror film and the pet sits next to you on the couch with the popcorn. Keep the phone in your hand and it feels you jump, or press the big button. When the credits roll it writes a review: "You jumped four times. Most of all at minute 31; I nearly dropped the popcorn."

## Good to know

**Does it die if I forget it?**

No. After a few days without fear it falls asleep in the wardrobe and smells of mothballs. It wakes up when you feed it.

**What leaves the phone?**

Only the search words, while you look a film or a book up. Everything you feed it stays on the phone.

## Get it

- **Requires:** Android 9.0 (API 28) or newer. No Google services needed.
- **Permissions:** internet (only for search), notifications (the evening knock before the watch) and vibration.
- **License:** MIT

1. Download the APK on your phone.
1. Open it and allow installs from this source when Android asks.
1. Look under the bed and feed it the last horror film you watched.
