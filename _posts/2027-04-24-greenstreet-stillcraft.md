I walk the same road to the metro every day and I couldn't tell you what grows along it. Farming games, on the other hand, I know by heart: which crop pays, which neighbour likes what, when the fair is.

Green Street puts the two together. The farm is your real district, drawn in bright pixel art from OpenStreetMap, and the beds go on the lawns by the roads you already walk. Walking past waters them. It's free, with no ads and no accounts, and it's a beta: everything works, and the balance is still being tuned on real walks.

## What's inside

### A letter from Aunt Klava

It starts with a fat envelope: a letter from your great-aunt, a key on a string and an old sweet tin full of seed packets. She has moved to the village and left you her hens, her yard and her habit of forty years: planting something living along the street. Then you mark your home on the map, and the game downloads and paints your district.

### Beds by the road

Stand by a lawn next to a pavement or a park path and «Sow» appears. A stake with a flag in the plant's colour marks the bed on the map. You water it by walking past: ten metres is enough at first, more with a better watering can. Rain waters for you. A bed left alone for three days goes wild, but it's never lost. When a sparkle shows, pick the harvest.

### Finds on the way

Every few hundred metres something turns up, depending on where you are, the month and the weather: morels after rain, rowan in frost, a driftwood branch by the water, a packet of seeds someone forgot on a bench, a groat by the kerb, a neighbour's lost mitten to take back. Sometimes it's just a moment: someone has chalked hopscotch all the way to the corner.

{% include figure.html path="blog/greenstreet-post/shot-1.jpg" caption="The bag: seeds and everything found on the way." %}

### Neighbours

They live in real places of your district: the bakery, the pharmacy, the library, the market. Each one gives seeds once a day, takes a present once a day and asks for help. The requests come one after another and add up to stories that last three years, with 735 requests and 474 letters in all.

{% include figure.html path="blog/greenstreet-post/shot-2.jpg" caption="Seeds, a request, a present." %}

### The yard

Hens that lay every day, pots on the windowsill, a kitchen with 95 recipes, the notice board, and the people of your yard, including Pryanik the ginger tom, who must not be fed sausage whatever he says. The yard is drawn after your real home: an old city courtyard, a block of flats or a house of your own.

{% include figure.html path="blog/greenstreet-post/shot-3.jpg" caption="The yard." %}

### The fair and the household

On Saturdays the market buys your harvest, and one crop is in demand every week at twice the price. The groats go on the household: eight upgrades, each changing one rule. More bed places or more eggs, mulch so beds wait a week, fleece against frost, a compost box, your own stall at the fair.

{% include figure.html path="blog/greenstreet-post/shot-4.jpg" caption="What the groats are for." %}

### Tasks instead of a manual

The first fourteen tasks, from Aunt Klava and the neighbours, show every mechanic one step at a time. After that the year turns by itself: festivals, a folk calendar for every day, a balcony and hives in the second year, a shared garden in the third.

{% include figure.html path="blog/greenstreet-post/shot-5.jpg" caption="Every mechanic, step by step." %}

## Good to know

**Does it work in a village?**

Yes. The yard follows your home, and the neighbours settle in the real places nearby.

**Does it drain the battery?**

GPS works while the game is open, or during a walk you start yourself, which shows a notification until you end it.

**Where does my position go?**

Nowhere. The map of your district is downloaded once from OpenFreeMap and kept on the phone, and the weather comes from MET Norway.

## Get it

- **Requires:** Android 9 (API 28) or newer, with GPS.
- **Permissions:** location, notifications for the walk, internet for the map and the weather.
- **License:** MIT. Map data © OpenStreetMap contributors. Font: m6x11.

1. Download the APK on your phone.
1. Open it and allow installs from this source when Android asks.
1. Read Aunt Klava's letter, mark your home and go for a walk.
