I play on a PC, on a console, on the phone and at a table with friends, and no single place remembers all of it. Steam knows its own hours, the board game shelf knows nothing, and three weeks after putting a long RPG aside I can't recall which quest I was in the middle of. Questling is a diary for all of it. You keep a small tavern at the crossroads of worlds, «The Last Save»; every session you play feeds the creatures by its hearth, and at night what they ate turns into growth.

It's free, with no ads and no account. Only two things ever leave the phone: a game's title when you look it up in the catalogue, and your town's coordinates, so the tavern window can show the real sky. This is the first version.

## What's inside

### Sessions

The big button starts a session: pick a game, a platform and who is playing, and the clock runs, in the background too. A session already played can be written down afterwards. At the end you note how it went – a win, a loss, a win together – everybody's score, a moment worth marking (a boss, a chapter, the credits, 100%), the mood and a line about where you stopped. That last line is the one I open the app for a month later.

{% include figure.html path="blog/questling-post/shot-1.jpg" caption="The journal: every session, day by day." %}

### Any game, with its cover

Video games, board games, card games, tabletop RPG campaigns, phone games. Type a title and the catalogue – Wikidata and Wikipedia – fills in the cover, the year, the maker and the genres. Each game has a page of its own: the hours, the status, your rating out of ten, the milestones, the people you played it with and every session.

{% include figure.html path="blog/questling-post/shot-4.jpg" caption="A game's page: hours, status, rating and where you stopped." %}

Screenshots take care of themselves: when a session ends, the app offers the screenshots the phone made during it, and photos of the table can come from the gallery or the phone's own camera. Phone games can be found for you as well. With usage access turned on, the app sees which games were on screen and for how long, and each stretch becomes a session with one tap.

### For the table

Dice from d4 to d100, up to eight at once. A fair «who goes first». A turn timer that buzzes when the time is up. A score sheet that works out who won and remembers it, so the players' page can tell you the score between you and each friend.

### 36 creatures

Some came out of game worlds – a slime, a mimic, a goblin merchant, a living twenty-sided die – others are ordinary animals with a gamepad in their paws. Apart from the questling you start with, each is found by the way you play: the slime for hours in RPGs, the meeple for board game nights, the necro cat for late sessions, the corgi for coming back to a game you had dropped. Every creature has six stats, and the games it was raised on give it a class and a favourite genre. Three live by the fire and double the gold for their thing.

{% include figure.html path="blog/questling-post/shot-3.jpg" caption="Creatures: three by the fire and the collection of 36." %}

### Trophies, quests and a season

Nearly 300 trophies in bronze, silver, gold and platinum. Most are ladders – sessions, hours, games finished, genres tried, wins, company – and a few dozen are secret. Three quests arrive every Monday, and a season lasts a quarter, with thirty levels of rewards. None of it can be bought.

{% include figure.html path="blog/questling-post/shot-2.jpg" caption="Trophies: ladders, and the ones you are close to." %}

### Rest pays

Play for a long time without a break and your pet yawns and suggests a stretch – once, without nagging. Breaks bring gold, and the hours past three a day feed the creatures less. Nobody starves when you don't play.

## Good to know

**How is it different from Mimic?**

Mimic is a shelf: what you own and what is still waiting to be played. Questling is a diary: every session, who you played with and how it went.

**Does it count the time by itself?**

On the phone, yes, if you allow usage access. For a PC, a console or a board game you start the clock yourself or write the session down later.

**It's the first version. What if something breaks?**

Please tell me: Settings → StillCraft has buttons to report a bug or suggest an idea through the StillCraft bot.

## Get it

- **Requires:** Android 9 (API 28) or newer.
- **Permissions:** internet for the game catalogue and the weather in the window; notifications for the session clock and the break reminder. Access to photos and to usage data is off until you turn it on. No account, no ads, no analytics.
- **License:** MIT. Fonts: Lora, Nunito and Inter (OFL). Catalogue by Wikidata and Wikipedia. Weather by Open-Meteo and MET Norway.

1. Download the APK on the phone.
1. Open it and allow installs from this source when Android asks.
1. Say what you play and start your first session.
