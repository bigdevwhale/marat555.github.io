Most of us carry a handful of rules we never chose. "If I ask for help, they'll think I'm weak." "Rest has to be earned." "If it isn't perfect, it's a failure." Cognitive behavioural therapy has good tools for loosening them: weigh the evidence, answer a few honest questions, test the rule in real life and see what happens. The tools work, but filling in thought records is about as exciting as filling in a tax form.

So I turned it into a heist. Your limiting beliefs are a bank called No, and every box in it keeps something you forbade yourself. Inside Job is free, with no ads, no account and no internet.

## What's inside

### The bank in cross-section

Floor 1 keeps the rules of the day: what you expect to happen at the meeting, on the date, at the gym. The vault below keeps the laws they come from, like "I must cope with everything alone". The central safe holds the core beliefs, the short, old ones. Red string shows what rests on what, and cracking a box lower down takes power from the alarms above it.

{% include figure.html path="blog/insidejob-post/shot-1.jpg" caption="The Bank of No, three floors and red string." %}

### A guard and a prize

Every box has a guard, who speaks in your own voice ("Do it yourself. Don't embarrass yourself."), and a prize: the right the belief took from you. The right to ask for help. The right to a day off without excuses. The right to a late start. There are 192 ready boxes in eight spheres, but your own words always fit better.

### Fourteen systems

The guard's security is made of thinking traps, each drawn as a device on the blueprint. Mind reading is a laser grid. Catastrophising is a siren that wails the same at a mouse and a fire. "Should" is a guard post with a dog. The hall of mirrors shows other people's shop windows next to your kitchen. Each system goes dark after three hits.

### The crew

- **The Pick** files evidence against the guard: one hit.
- **The Professor**, an old safecracker, taps a question through the wall, and an honest answer clicks the lock: one hit. There are 304 questions, each aimed at its own trap.
- **The Driver** takes you on a job, a small real-life test. Before it you write down the guard's forecast and how sure it is; after it, what actually happened, and you stamp the folder. A quiet alarm is two hits.
- **The Shadow** notes when the guard speaks, until it's clear it works Monday mornings.
- **The Quill** finds out who passed the old verdict, and when, and rewrites it in a grown-up hand.

### What's under this?

From any box you can go down a floor by asking: if this were true, what would it say about me? That's how a rule of the day leads to the law under it, and the law to the central safe.

### Cracking the safe

When the last alarm goes dark and the recon is done, the dial spins, the door swings, and the loot goes into a velvet case with your new rule engraved on it. If the guard comes back one day, the box locks again, but one hit stays in every hall.

## Good to know

**Is this therapy?**

No. It borrows the methods of cognitive behavioural therapy and turns them into a game, but it doesn't diagnose or treat anything. If things are hard, a therapist is the right person to talk to. Words about self-harm open a page of helplines instead of the heist.

**What leaves the phone?**

Nothing. The app has no internet permission at all.
