Some evenings I look back at the day and can't name a single thing I did. Then I think about it properly. I washed the pan that had been sitting there since last night. I called my grandma before she called me. I went to bed before midnight for once. None of it is an achievement, and to-do apps and habit trackers have no place for it: they want a plan in the morning and a streak by the evening, and the streak breaks the first time you're ill.

So I made an app that does the opposite. Nothing is planned. In the evening a commission sits, you tick what happened, and for each small deed you get an enamel pin badge, the old Soviet kind, with an order typed out, a rubber stamp and a fanfare. Free, no ads, no account, no internet.

## What's inside

### The commission sits

In the evening you open the agenda. It lists deeds you've done before, deeds that fit the hour and the season, and a few you've never been honoured for. You tick what happened and put them forward for awards. Nothing was promised in the morning, so nothing can be failed, and there are no streaks to break.

The list has 337 deeds in seven ministries: Household, Body, People, Out & about, Courage, Mind & hands, and my favourite, the **Ministry of Deserved Idleness**. It honours what you *didn't* do: you didn't answer the work chat after hours, you lay down for twenty minutes without your phone, you went to bed at nine just because you could.

### The ceremony

Each badge rises on red velvet to a short brass fanfare. Then the order is typed out in chancellery style, *"For unparalleled fortitude displayed in washing a pan left overnight…"*, and the blue stamp comes down. Tilt the phone and the gloss slides across the enamel. It's a joke, of course, but a warm one, and the deed it's about is real.

{% include figure.html path="blog/honours-post/shot-2.jpg" caption="The Ten Pages Medal, for reading ten pages amid universal busyness." %}

### Classes and the pennant

Do the same deed again and the badge grows: bronze for Class III, silver at 5 awards, gold at 15, and the Honoured title at 30. New badges are pinned to a red felt pennant with a gold fringe, and you can hold one to move it. After 16 a new pennant goes up and the old ones stay on the wall.

### The Order of the Day Survived

Some days there's nothing to tick. On those the commission awards an order simply for getting to the evening. There are secret badges too, which find you by themselves: the Night Watch, the Early Bird, the Return after a month of silence, the Shock Worker of Small Labour for five awards in a day.

{% include figure.html path="blog/honours-post/shot-3.jpg" caption="For seeing this day through to its end, however it went." %}

### The workshop and award sheets

If the list doesn't have your deed, institute a badge of your own: pick a shape and two enamels, take a stamped picture or draw one on a 9×9 grid, and write the decree, with chancellery openers like "For exemplary…" to start it. And small deeds aren't only yours. Honour a friend, a colleague or your mum, and the commission draws up an award sheet with the badge, the order, your signature and a blue stamp, sent as a picture.

## Good to know

**Isn't it silly to get a medal for washing a pan?**

A little, and that's the point. The chancellery tone is a joke, but on most days small deeds are the only ones there are, and they deserve to be noticed.

**Will it nag me?**

Once at most. At the hour you choose there is one quiet notification, "The commission is sitting", and only on a day that has no award yet.

## Get it

- **Requires:** Android 9.0 (API 28) or newer. No Google services needed.
- **Permissions:** notifications and vibration. No internet.
- **License:** MIT

1. Download the APK on your phone.
1. Open it and allow installs from this source when Android asks.
1. In the evening, open the commission and tick what happened today.
