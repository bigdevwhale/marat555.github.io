I watch a lot of films, and a week later I couldn't tell you which. The trackers I tried felt like spreadsheets: a title, a score, done. I wanted the evening itself to leave something behind.

Reelkin turns it into a tiny picture house. Something sleeps in a film can in the projectionist's booth, and every film you watch feeds it, seats a spectator in your hall and puts a letter on your cinema's sign. It's free, with no ads and no accounts, and everything stays on the phone.

## What's inside

### The film can

Log your first three films and the can on the booth floor shakes, the lid flies off and someone climbs out. The genres decide who: noir gives a cat, horror or sci-fi a bat, animation a fox, comedies a raccoon, dramas an owlet. One in sixteen comes out in technicolor.

### Showtime

Put a film on the projector: the lights go down, the reels turn and a beam goes through the port into the hall. The pet sits by the port and watches with you, jumping at horror, laughing at comedy and insisting it's only dust in the beam during a romance. When the running time is up, the credits roll and you tear a stub.

{% include figure.html path="blog/reelkin-post/shot-1.jpg" caption="Now showing." %}

### The stub

Popcorn from one to five, three words about the film, who you watched it with, and whether there was a scene after the credits. Then the rewards come one by one: a spectator takes a seat, a new letter for the sign, a new decade, a country stamp. Every minute you watch helps the pet grow, and the third film of a genre gives it something to keep: a fedora from noir, an antenna from sci-fi, a red nose from comedy, neck bolts from horror.

{% include figure.html path="blog/reelkin-post/shot-3.jpg" caption="What one film brings." %}

### The hall

A hundred velvet seats, seen from the back row. They fill with detectives, cowboys, ghosts and astronauts, dressed by the genres you watch. Tap one and it shows its stub. Below the hall are the sign (your cinema's name, built only from the first letters of your films), a time machine of decades, country stamps, post-credits scenes and the year's awards you hand out yourself.

{% include figure.html path="blog/reelkin-post/shot-2.jpg" caption="The hall and the sign." %}

### Series on a season ticket

An episode tears a ticket from the booklet. «Cliffhanger!» marks the worst moment in red, and the pet won't sleep until the next one. A season finale seats a spectator. A series you've dropped is simply taken off, with no guilt and no counter.

{% include figure.html path="blog/reelkin-post/shot-5.jpg" caption="Season tickets, films to come and stubs." %}

### What's on tonight?

Say how much time you have and what you're in the mood for. The pet picks from your bill what fits, and prefers a decade or a country you don't have yet. Ask for a double bill and it finds two that belong together. If only half an hour is left, it suggests an episode.

{% include figure.html path="blog/reelkin-post/shot-4.jpg" caption="Time and mood." %}

## Good to know

**Where do the posters come from?**

From Wikipedia, found through Wikidata when you search. You can show them in pixels to match the booth or as they are; the booth and the hall stay pixel either way.

**What if I stop watching for a month?**

Nothing happens. The pet doesn't starve and nobody sulks. It's a diary, not a duty.

## Get it

- **Requires:** Android 9 (API 28) or newer.
- **Permissions:** internet, only for the film search.
- **License:** MIT. Fonts: Prata, PT Sans Narrow, Manrope.

1. Download the APK on your phone.
1. Open it and allow installs from this source when Android asks.
1. Log the last three films you watched and see who climbs out of the can.
