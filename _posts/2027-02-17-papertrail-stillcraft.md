I've started a budget app a dozen times, and every time it ended the same way. A pie chart told me I spent too much on food, a red number told me I was over, and a week later I stopped opening it. Charts only tell you *what* happened. What I wanted to know was *why*: why the delivery always came after ten, why Friday cost twice as much as Tuesday. That's not accounting. That's an investigation.

Paper Trail is a detective's office in a noir town where it has rained for three weeks, and the client is you. Spending is evidence, your categories are suspects with police photos, and when the clues show a pattern a case opens. You name the motive and hand down the verdict. No shame, just detective work. Free, no ads, no account, and nothing leaves the phone.

## What's inside

### Today's advance, on a matchbook

You name a monthly sum for living and your payday: the client's advance. The matchbook on the desk shows what may still go today. It takes what's left of the advance after your known bills and spreads it over the days to the next fee. There's one match for each day of the period, and the past ones are burnt. Spend less today and tomorrow's figure grows; go over and it quietly shrinks.

### Clues, and an informant

Every spend is a clue. Type it in on the typewriter, an amount and a face, or take on the **informant**: it reads your bank's notifications on the phone and leaves an envelope on the desk with the sum and the place. It only listens to apps you mark as banks, and there's no internet permission at all. Once a suspect has been to the same place twice, its envelopes sort themselves.

{% include figure.html path="blog/papertrail-post/shot-3.jpg" caption="An envelope from the informant: one tap on a face files it." %}

### Suspects with faces

Categories are faces in the files, each police photo drawn in code with a hat, a giveaway detail and hard noir light. The Night Courier is food delivery after ten. The Box Man is online shops on sale days. The Barista takes a little but every day. The Maître d' likes Fridays, and the Cabbie works the rain. Groceries, rent, the doctor and transport are *honest citizens*: watched, but never put on a case. Each dossier shows the record month by month, the hours and weekdays a suspect works, and the tail you can put on them.

{% include figure.html path="blog/papertrail-post/shot-2.jpg" caption="The files: suspects above, honest citizens below." %}

### Cases open by themselves

There are five patterns:
- night work after ten;
- death by small change;
- a sudden spree against the usual week;
- the same weekday every week;
- a broken tail.

When one shows up, a case opens on its own. Its clues hang on a cork board, tied with red thread to the suspect's photo.

### Motive and verdict

You name the motive: tiredness, boredom, a treat, a sale, for company, or it was needed. Then you acquit, or find guilty and pick a measure: keep an eye, set a monthly tail, or ban for a week. The stamp comes down. An acquittal counts as a solved case too, because a spend you chose isn't a crime.

{% include figure.html path="blog/papertrail-post/shot-1.jpg" caption="Case no. 7: five late clues, all from the Night Courier." %}

### Ghosts and cold cases

Subscriptions find themselves: the same sum from the same place about a month apart becomes a ghost, and it tells you what it eats in a year. Exorcise it when you cancel, and the informant tells you if it ever comes back. Cash taken out with no trace is a cold case until you remember what it went on.

### The safe, and tickets out of town

Put leftover money in the safe. A ticket out of this town is a savings goal with ten stamp boxes, filled from the safe until it's paid in full.

{% include figure.html path="blog/papertrail-post/shot-5.jpg" caption="The safe and two one-way tickets." %}

### The city paper

Each period gets a front page of *The City Ledger*. It says who was caught and who's at large, then runs the figures, the crime beat and the ghosts in town. At nine in the evening, a typed line from the detective's diary sums up the day.

{% include figure.html path="blog/papertrail-post/shot-4.jpg" caption="The front page: the Cabbie caught, spending on taxis down 74%." %}

## Good to know

**Will it scold me?**

No. Your rank grows with cases closed and cold cases solved, never with spending less, and honest citizens are never prosecuted. The point is to see why the money goes. You still decide where it should go.

**Does it connect to my bank?**

No. The informant reads only the notifications your bank already shows on the phone, from apps you mark yourself. If you don't want it, type clues in by hand and everything else works the same.
