The best part of staying in someone else's flat abroad was never the sights. It was the kitchen drawer with the strange whisk in it, the record collection, the shelf of books you couldn't read, and the note on the fridge: "the good coffee is in the top cupboard; the market is better on Thursdays". For a week you lived inside somebody else's ordinary life.

Under the Mat is that week, from home. Twelve people in twelve cities have gone away for seven days and left you their flat, and a note in every corner. It's free, with no ads and no account.

## What's inside

### The noticeboard

Flats for a week are pinned to a cork board: Naples, Kyoto, Oaxaca, Bergen, Seoul, Lyon, Tbilisi, Kolkata, Salvador, Lisbon, Galway, Istanbul. Each one belongs to a person, not a country. Nonna Lucia in Naples pretends she doesn't feed the neighbour's cat. Ōta Shizuko, a retired teacher in an old Kyoto townhouse, writes her notes as numbered lists. Arne in Bergen owns eleven umbrellas. Ji-ho in Seoul has gone to his grandmother's farm to make the winter kimchi and left the door code on a sticky note under the mat.

{% include figure.html path="blog/undermat-post/shot-1.jpg" caption="Flats for a week." %}

### What every local knows

That was the rule for every flat: not what tourists know, but what everybody there grew up with. The film shown on television every New Year. The song that ends up being sung at every wedding. The novel from the school syllabus that everybody groaned through and can still quote. The dish a grandmother makes on Sunday. The fridge magnet with a fact you'd never find in a guidebook, and a word of the day in the local language, said aloud by the phone's own voice.

### Notes in every corner

The flat is one room drawn in code, and every corner holds a note in the host's handwriting. The kitchen has tonight's recipe, with what to use if your supermarket doesn't sell it, and a shopping list to send to yourself. The television has a film and why it matters there; the record player, an album; the bookshelf, a book. "Where to find" just opens a search in the services you already use: the app knows only titles. There's a house rule under the bread, a word that doesn't translate in a frame on the wall, and a letter in the desk drawer.

{% include figure.html path="blog/undermat-post/shot-2.jpg" caption="Tonight's recipe, in Nonna's hand." %}

### One day at a time

A new day of notes opens each morning, for seven days. The letters in the drawer tell a small story across the week: why the host went away, their family, a memory, and, around day five or six, a little confession. Nonna Lucia does feed the cat.

### A real window

Each city's flat has its own floor, tiles, window, lamp and one object that could only be there: a red horn on the fridge in Naples, a glass wind chime in Kyoto, paper bunting in Oaxaca, a drinking horn on the wall in Tbilisi, a ceiling fan in Kolkata. Outside the window is the host's city at its local time, under the real sunrise, sunset and weather. Opening the flat in Bergen on a dry evening at home to find it raining there, as it does, was the moment I knew the app worked.

### Neighbours

On days two, four and six somebody drops by. In Naples the woman upstairs lowers a basket on a rope past the window with a sfogliatella in it; in Bergen a boy knocks with a raffle ticket; in Seoul the landlady brings rice cake for the new tenant. Each visit comes with the custom behind it.

### An evening, and the suitcase

The evening card puts a day on one table for two or for friends: the dish, the record while it cooks, the film after dinner, the toast, and three things to talk about. Everything cooked, watched, heard or begun becomes a luggage sticker in the city's colours on an old suitcase. On day seven you leave the host something of your own country, a dish, a film, a song and a note, and put the key back under the mat. The week stays in "My weeks" to read again.

## Good to know

**Are the hosts real?**

No, the hosts and their lives are invented. Every film, record, book, dish and holiday is real.

**Can I read the whole week at once?**

No, one day opens each morning, as if you were really staying. After the week every note can be read again.

**Does it play the films and music?**

No. "Where to find" opens a search in your own services; the app shows and plays nothing itself.

**What leaves the phone?**

Only the city's coordinates, for the weather outside the host's window.

## Get it

- **Requires:** Android 9.0 (API 28) or newer. No Google services needed.
- **Permissions:** notifications, for one quiet note in the evening; internet, for the weather.
- **License:** MIT.

1. Download the APK on your phone.
1. Open it and allow installs from this source when Android asks.
1. Pick a flat on the board, lift the mat and open the door.
