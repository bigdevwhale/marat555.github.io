I started with a list. Cosy rituals for every day: go to a bookshop and leaf through books, make tea in a proper pot, walk in the rain. It turned out dull within a minute. A list of cosy things is still a list of things to do, and choosing from it is a small chore of its own.

The cosy moments I actually remember were never planned. The kettle starting to whistle. Rain on the umbrella on the way home. A lamp in the evening while everyone else is asleep. They were already there; I only had to notice them. So Nooklings doesn't suggest anything. It catches what's already happening, rolls it into a jar, and lets six small lodgers eat it. It's free, with no ads and no account.

## What's inside

### The net

There is one big button, «Catch something cosy», and four ways to use it. **Sound** records ten seconds from the microphone: hold the phone near the kettle, the rain, the laughter in the kitchen. **Light** takes a photo with the phone's own camera, and the jar fills with its colour, amber for a lamp, violet for a dusk. **Quiet** asks you to put the phone down for three minutes; it measures only the loudness and starts over if the room gets noisy. **Steps** comes from the step counter, and walks can be caught on their own.

{% include figure.html path="blog/nooklings-post/shot-1.jpg" caption="Four ways to catch." %}

### One tap, one jar

Then one tap for what it was: weather, a walk, tea and food, warmth, something new, people, something made by hand. The moment is rolled into a jar with a gingham cloth over the lid and a label in handwriting, the date, the town and the weather it was caught in. The jars stand on the pantry shelves and glow at night.

{% include figure.html path="blog/nooklings-post/shot-2.jpg" caption="The pantry." %}

### Six lodgers

The room belongs to six small creatures, and each eats its own kind of cosiness. The cat on the stove eats warmth and tea. The dormouse in a teacup eats quiet. The moth in round glasses on the lampshade eats anything new: a bookshop, a street you've never walked down. The bullfinch on the windowsill eats voices. The hedgehog in a felt boot eats weather and walks. The raccoon in the yarn basket eats things made by hand.

Only the cat and the dormouse live there at first. The others knock at the window the first time you catch their kind: catch some laughter, and that night the bullfinch flies in.

{% include figure.html path="blog/nooklings-post/shot-3.jpg" caption="Who eats what." %}

### Nobody dies

I didn't want another app that punishes you for a busy week. A hungry lodger goes pale and sleeps in a knitted scarf, and one jar of its kind wakes it up. Full ones glow. In the evening, when everyone is full, they climb down and pile up on the rug by the stove, the bullfinch on top of the raccoon's head.

### Dreams

Now and then a lodger dreams a riddle. "The moth dreams of the smell of old paper." How you answer it is up to you. When a jar of that kind arrives, the dream comes true, and the lodger leaves a gift that stays in the room for good: a rug, a candle, a postcard from somewhere new, a sprig of rowan on the windowsill, a maple leaf on the wall, a string of lights over the window.

{% include figure.html path="blog/nooklings-post/shot-4.jpg" caption="The moth is dreaming." %}

### Open a jar

This is the part I made the whole thing for. On a hard day, open a jar. It plays what it caught, your own ten seconds of that rain or that kettle. Jars without a recording still sound like their kind: a light jar crackles like the stove, a quiet jar has a clock ticking in it. The fire flares up and everyone gathers by the stove to listen.

{% include figure.html path="blog/nooklings-post/shot-5.jpg" caption="First snow, a rare one." %}

### Jam and rare jars

Two jars can be cooked into jam, a new jar of both kinds that feeds both lodgers. Jars caught in snow or a thunderstorm come out rare and glow brighter, and the first snow of the season gets a jar of its own.

### A real window

Like the other pixel rooms on this shelf, it's drawn entirely in code: masks lit from the top left, coloured outlines, a lamp and a fire that light the room pixel by pixel at night. The window shows your town at its real time of day and in its real weather.

{% include figure.html path="blog/nooklings-post/shot-6.jpg" caption="Daytime." %}

## Good to know

**Does it listen all the time?**

No. The microphone is on only for the ten seconds you catch, or during a quiet catch, and then only the loudness is measured.

**What if I forget about it for a week?**

The lodgers will be asleep in scarves. That's all.

**What leaves the phone?**

Only your town's coordinates, for the weather in the window. The sounds and photos stay in the app's own folder.

## Get it

- **Requires:** Android 9.0 (API 28) or newer. No Google services needed.
- **Permissions:** microphone, for sound and the quiet meter; physical activity, for the step counter; internet, for the weather.
- **License:** MIT.

1. Download the APK on your phone.
1. Open it and allow installs from this source when Android asks.
1. Next time something is cosy, press «Catch something cosy».
