Every few weeks I find a kefir at the back of the fridge that went off days ago, and a box of pills that expired last spring. Expiry trackers exist, but they are spreadsheets with a calendar: you dutifully type in a dozen yogurts, then stop opening the app. I wanted keeping the fridge in order to feel like looking after something, not like doing paperwork.

Apothecary turns the fridge, the cupboard, the freezer and the medicine box into a cosy potion shop. Every product is a bottle of essence that glows while it's fresh and dims as its date comes near. Cooking a meal is brewing a potion. It's free, with no ads, no accounts and no internet at all.

## What's inside

### Shelves and the real deadline

There are four places: the cool cellar (the fridge), the pantry (the cupboard), the ice chest (the freezer) and the cabinet behind the counter (the medicine box). Each shows what runs out first at the top.

The deadline isn't just the printed date. It's the date on the package or the day the thing was opened plus the days it keeps once open, whichever comes first. Milk dated two weeks ahead lasts three days once opened, and its bottle says so. The ice chest stops the old clock and starts a new, longer one; take something out to thaw and it keeps a day or two.

A guide of about 170 products fills all this in: type "kef…" and kefir lands on its shelf with its date and three days once open. Leftovers get a number to write on the lid, so you never guess which soup it is.

{% include figure.html path="blog/apothecary-post/shot-1.jpg" caption="The cellar: the fridge, by urgency." %}

### The cauldron

Tick what went into the meal and the cauldron shows which potion it will make before you brew. Forty recipes come from the mix of essences: dairy and berries make the Potion of Morning Clarity, meat and vegetables the Campfire Pot Potion, fish and lemon the Tincture of the Quiet Harbour. Five different essences make the Grand Elixir, and three things saved on their last day make the Potion of the Last Hour. The book keeps them all, and it's a gentle nudge to cook with what's about to be lost.

{% include figure.html path="blog/apothecary-post/shot-2.jpg" caption="The cauldron: the potion before you brew." %}

### Customers, coins and the guild

Six regulars come in each morning: the herbalist, the miller, a retired knight, the clockmaker, a bard and the ferrywoman. Each asks for something brewed from exactly the essences that are on their last days, and the note says which of your bottles will do. Potions bring coins, more for things saved on their last day and for orders, and the coins fit out the shop: candles, herbs from the beams, a black cat on the counter, an owl, a stained-glass window, a copper cauldron. Guild ranks go from Apprentice to Guildmaster and depend on how little was poured out in the last thirty days.

{% include figure.html path="blog/apothecary-post/shot-3.jpg" caption="The book: forty recipes to find." %}

### Order and numbers

The shop's summary names what's on its last day today and tomorrow and what has gone murky, with "pour out" and "ate it anyway" right on the row. The calendar shows which day each thing runs out, and what was brewed or poured out on past days. The ledger counts potions, things saved, things poured out and the money they cost, with eight weeks in bars and what goes to waste most often. Once a week the shop suggests a night audit: walk the shelves and mark what's gone. The morning bell names what's due today and tomorrow and stays silent otherwise.

{% include figure.html path="blog/apothecary-post/shot-4.jpg" caption="A bottle up close: opened, keeps three days." %}

### The medicine box

The cabinet behind the counter keeps the guild's kit: something for fever, for pain, an antiseptic, bandages, a sorbent, something for allergy and heartburn, a thermometer. It shows what's missing or out of date, warns a month ahead, keeps the short life of opened drops and syrups, counts vitamin courses, and sends expired medicines to a pharmacy collection box rather than the bin.

{% include figure.html path="blog/apothecary-post/shot-5.jpg" caption="The calendar: which day each thing runs out." %}

## Good to know

**Do I have to type in every yogurt?**

Only the name; the guide fills in the rest. Start with what spoils fast (dairy, meat, greens, leftovers) and add rice and pasta later.

**Are the guide's dates reliable?**

They are ordinary household advice, rounded to the careful side. The package always knows better, and any date can be changed.

## Get it

- **Requires:** Android 9 (API 28) or newer.
- **Permissions:** notifications for the morning bell. No internet permission at all.
- **License:** MIT. Fonts: Cormorant, Alegreya Sans.

1. Download the APK on the phone.
1. Open it and allow installs from this source when Android asks.
1. Tap "+" and put up what's in the fridge, starting with what spoils soonest.
