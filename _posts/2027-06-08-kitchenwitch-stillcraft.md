Most of us cook the same dozen dishes on rotation. Kitchen Witch is a recipe book built to break that loop gently. You are already a witch, and your witchcraft is cooking. There are no teachers and no homework: the real moon keeps the rhythm, and once a moon you set yourself a dish you have never made.

It's free, with no ads and no account. The only thing that leaves the phone is your town's coordinates, so the kitchen window can show the real weather. This is the first version.

## What's inside

### The kitchen window

The home screen is an arched window onto a pine forest. The moon outside is in its real phase, the weather is your town's – fog, rain, snow, a flash of lightning in a storm – and fireflies drift between the trees at night. On the sill a cauldron glows green and steams, candles flicker, and a jar of fireflies blinks.

Below it is the spell of this moon: the one new dish you've promised yourself.

### The moon's rhythm

The moon splits the month into four moods. At the new moon you set a dish that isn't in your book yet. As it waxes, you try something that isn't on your shelf. The full moon is for guests – they sign the book. As it wanes, you use up what has been waiting in the fridge.

{% include figure.html path="blog/kitchenwitch-post/shot-1.jpg" caption="The moon's ring: begin, try, sabbat, clear." %}

### The bones

When you can't decide what's for dinner, throw the bones. They pick a dish you've never cooked – in season first – or only one that uses the leftovers in your pantry. Three throws a day; a witch doesn't dither.

### Cooking

Before you start, you can choose an intention: for courage, for calm, for love. Then the steps come one at a time. When a step names a time – "simmer for 20 minutes" – a candle timer appears next to it, and a bell rings when the time is up. The screen stays on while you cook.

At the end you note how it went, write something in the margin, add who was at the table and photograph the plate with the phone's camera. Then the dish's sigil draws itself in gold.

{% include figure.html path="blog/kitchenwitch-post/shot-2.jpg" caption="Before the spell: an intention and everything you'll need." %}

### The book of shadows

Every dish lands in the book with its photo, its note and a sigil drawn from its ingredients, so no two look alike. Scrolls are recipes you've written down but not cooked yet – their sigils stay pale. Misfires are kept too, and making one good earns a mark.

{% include figure.html path="blog/kitchenwitch-post/shot-3.jpg" caption="The book of shadows." %}

Recipes come from a built-in library of 59 dishes in English and Russian, from a link (most recipe sites publish markup the app can read), from a photo of a cookbook page or a grandmother's notebook, from your own words, or from memory after you've already cooked.

### The shelf and the wheel

The shelf holds 42 jars, each with an old kitchen belief: rosemary for remembrance, bay leaves for wishes. The first time a spice appears in something you cook, its jar takes its place on the shelf.

{% include figure.html path="blog/kitchenwitch-post/shot-4.jpg" caption="The shelf, and the places still empty." %}

The wheel of the year has the eight feasts, from Yule to Samhain, each with its own menu. And there are 22 witch's marks you can't see in advance – for cooking in a thunderstorm, baking bread at the full moon, supper after midnight.

{% include figure.html path="blog/kitchenwitch-post/shot-5.jpg" caption="The wheel of the year." %}

## Good to know

**Do I have to believe in magic?**

No. The moon and the marks are just a reason to cook something new more often. The recipes are perfectly ordinary.

**It's the first version. What if something breaks?**

Please tell me: the settings (the gear in the kitchen) have buttons to report a bug or suggest an idea through the StillCraft bot.

## Get it

- **Permissions:** internet only, for the weather and recipe links. No account, no ads, no analytics.
- **License:** MIT. Fonts: Cormorant Garamond, Inter and Caveat (OFL). Weather by Open-Meteo and MET Norway.
