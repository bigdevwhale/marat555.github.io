Some of the best conversations happen on long journeys, with nowhere to go and nothing to scroll. I wanted to bottle that feeling for an ordinary evening at home.

People tell a stranger on a train what they never tell those closest to them. Night Train gives a couple that evening. The phone lies on the table between you and splits in two, each half turned to its reader, with two windows back to back in the middle. A polite conductor asks the questions, and they deepen through the evening, the night, a tunnel and the dawn. Stations with rules, fellow passengers who sit down for a while, a restaurant car. Free, no ads, no account, no internet permission.

## What's inside

### The phone is the table

Sitting face to face, the screen splits in two: the top half turned to the person opposite, the bottom half to you, and two windows back to back between them. Every card shows on both halves, with its buttons, so either of you can answer, skip or go on.

### Evening, night, tunnel, dawn

Each trip starts light and playful, turns personal at night and ends warm at dawn. The gradual, mutual openness follows Arthur Aron's study of how people grow close. Into the tunnel only if both hold a thumb down; otherwise the train goes round, and nobody asks why.

{% include figure.html path="blog/nighttrain-post/shot-1.jpg" caption="The station. A note waits in the compartment." %}

### 658 cards, never the same twice

359 conductor's questions over 12 subjects, 96 from fellow passengers, 45 whistle-stop actions, 90 restaurant-car dilemmas, 32 follow-ups for "deeper" and an 18+ sleeping car that stays off unless you switch it on. No question comes back until its whole stage has been heard.

### A railway to explore

16 cities and 27 lines across eight landscapes, from pine forest and the coast to snowfields and city lights. Each is its own view from the window and tilts the conductor toward its subjects: the forest brings back childhood, the coast brings out dreams. Each trip leaves from where the last one arrived.

{% include figure.html path="blog/nighttrain-post/shot-2.jpg" caption="Guess first. Say what the other will answer." %}

### Stations and fellow passengers

Nine stations with rules, from Still Water's ten seconds of silence to Crooked Lanes, where one answer is a secret lie. An old sailor, a fortune teller, a granny with pies, a silent monk who wants answers without words, the ticket inspector's five-second rapid fire. Whistle-stops and a restaurant car where you both show one finger or two.

### Guess each other

Say out loud what the other will answer: a hit punches a hole in the ticket, a miss is something new about the person opposite. The ticket book shows whether you're getting better at it, John Gottman's "love maps". Notes wait folded in the compartment until the next boarding, and a postcard you kept comes back a month later: the same train.

## Good to know

**Why a train?**

Because people tell a fellow traveller what they never tell those closest to them. The longer two people are together, the more they assume they already know each other; for one evening the train makes you strangers again.

**What if a question is too much?**

Pull the emergency brake and the card is skipped without a word. Either of you can take the train round the tunnel, no reason needed, and the 18+ sleeping car is off unless you switch it on.

**How long is a trip?**

Three trains: the Local takes about 25 minutes, the Express about an hour, and the Night train the whole evening.

**Where do our answers go?**

Only on the phone. No account, no cloud, no analytics. A backup goes to one file you keep yourself.
