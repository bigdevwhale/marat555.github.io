Most training logs are spreadsheets with streaks on top. They count, they chart, and the moment you miss a week they make you feel it. What they rarely show is the thing that actually matters over months: whether you train evenly. A runner who never lifts, a lifter who never stretches. The numbers look great right up to the injury.

So I gave the numbers a body. Aloft is a training log where every kind of training feeds an elemental spirit, and five spirits together hold a small island up above the clouds. It's free, with no ads and no account.

## What's inside

### Five spirits

Long and steady work feeds the sylph of air: running, walking, skiing, cycling. Heavy work feeds the golem of earth: weights, the pull-up bar, climbing. Anything in water feeds the undine. Stretching, yoga and balance feed the dryad. Bursts and fights feed the salamander of fire: intervals, boxing, ball games, dancing. There are 24 kinds built in, and you can add your own and give them to whichever spirit you think fits. My climbing gym went to the golem.

Each spirit grows from a blinking spark to an ember, a spirit and a great spirit, and the great ones get something extra: the golem a crystal, the undine pearls, the dryad little antlers of twigs.

### An honest load

A training counts as minutes times how hard it felt, from 1 to 10. Sports scientists call it session RPE; here it's the light that flies from your hand to the spirit after you log it. It fades over about two weeks, so a spirit shows what you've been doing lately, not what you did once in January. The island floats as high as the average of all five.

### Imbalance is weather

This is the part I like most. Lean too hard on one element and you see it on the island, not in a chart. Fire without water, and the springs dry up and the grass goes yellow. Water without earth, and the edges crumble. All running and no lifting, and gusts toss the island about. With no stretching at all, the stone cracks. Two strong elements together build something: a mill from air and earth, a forge from fire and earth, hot springs from water and fire, a garden, a grove, a rainbow.

When the last week's load jumps far above the last four weeks', a storm gathers and the spirit asks you to rest. That ratio is a real warning sign for injuries, and a thunderstorm is a friendlier way to say it than a red number. A rest day brings calm and sleeping spirits. If you're ill or injured, you drop anchor, and nothing fades until you weigh it.

### The voyage

Every training, of any kind, also carries the island along a season's route: the Goose Rookery, Laputa, Buyan, the Lighthouse of Winds, the Glass Mountain, the Rainbow Fair and, at the end, Avalon. The next season goes to Belovodye, Hyperborea and World's End. Each stop sends a short letter and a keepsake that appears on your island: a goose feather on the flagpole, a spyglass, an oak sapling from Buyan, a kite that never comes down.

### Islanders and guests

The island has people on it, and every Monday three of them ask for something small. The first request always comes from the element you've neglected: the herbalist wants an hour in the water to fill the pond, the stonemason needs a heavy stone for the steps. The lighthouse keeper sometimes just asks you to take a day off.

With a town set, the island sees the real weather, and some training brings guests. Run in the rain and Drizzle, a little cloud with a temper, comes to water the garden. Snow brings an owl, fog a shy Mistling, frost a tiny Jack Frost. Train before eight and a robin sings; after nine at night a firefly the size of an apple lights the paths. Try something new and the Wanderer turns up with his lantern.

### The council

At the end of the week the spirits sit round a fire and tell the week in a few plain sentences: who blazed, who got bored, what the island needs, how far it sailed.

## Good to know

**Does it read my watch or count steps?**

Not yet. Logging takes a few taps. An import from Health Connect is on the list.

**What if I only ever do one thing?**

Then the island shows it, and that's all. Nothing is taken away and nothing falls: a quiet month just lets the island sink into the clouds until the next walk lifts it again.

**What leaves the phone?**

Only the town search and the weather, which sends nothing but your town's coordinates. The log stays on the phone. The load model is a general guide, not medical advice.
