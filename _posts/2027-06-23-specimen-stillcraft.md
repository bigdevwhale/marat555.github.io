A friend with a cat once said her cat behaves like an alien sent to study us – so why not study it back? Specimen takes that literally. Your cat, dog or parrot is an envoy from another planet, and you are the xenologist assigned to observe it. You log its mood, appetite and weight, photograph what it does, try new foods as small experiments, and every Sunday the bureau files a typed report with your photos, captions and conclusions.

Under the joke it's a proper pet diary: care reminders made for the breed, red flags that mean «see a vet today», and a PDF extract to bring to the clinic. It's free, with no ads, no account and no internet access at all. This is the first version.

## What's inside

### Six home planets, 135 breeds

Registration starts with the home planet: cats, dogs, parrots and cage birds, rabbits and rodents, ferrets, reptiles. Then the breed – 135 of them, plus a «local subspecies» for mixed and unknown. Each breed carries its usual temperament, its normal weight and the health problems it tends to have, with a line on what to watch for: a British Shorthair's heart, a dachshund's back, a pug's breathing.

### One tap a day

Four round buttons on the station log the most common things: mood, appetite, weight and a photo. Mood is read from tail, ears and pose, appetite from four small engraved scenes – a pet asking for more, walking off content, sniffing without interest, turning its back on a full bowl. Symptoms are a list; the ones that can't wait, like a cat that can't pee or a rabbit that stopped eating, are marked, and the station says plainly when it's time for a vet.

{% include figure.html path="blog/specimen-post/shot-1.jpg" caption="The file: character against the breed's norm, predispositions and the passport." %}

### Experiments and the ethics committee

Anything new is an experiment: a food, a medicine, a move, guests, fireworks outside. You write what you expect – will like it, won't care, won't like it – and after a few days how it went and what you noticed. Foods add up to a diet ledger: what your pet loves and what it won't touch. Every application goes past the ethics committee first, which turns down the cucumber prank, punishment, hunger and anything poisonous for the species, from chocolate and grapes to lilies and human painkillers.

### The behaviour atlas

Two hundred poses and habits across the six planets: belly up, the loaf, the slow blink, the box nap, the play bow. Tag one on a photo and it opens in the atlas with your photo as its picture and what it really means – belly up is trust, not an invitation to rub. Rare entries take months to catch.

{% include figure.html path="blog/specimen-post/shot-2.jpg" caption="The atlas: poses opened by your own photos." %}

### The Sunday report

Every week becomes a report: a typed sheet with the photos of the week and their captions («First recorded: the creature occupied a cardboard module»), the behavioural profile and how it moved, weight against the breed, experiments, care, and two or three conclusions. Share it as a picture; three more designs are bought with grants.

{% include figure.html path="blog/specimen-post/shot-3.jpg" caption="A weekly report, typed on paper." %}

### The xenology course

Forty-five short lessons about body language, stress, play, food and health, each with a task to try with your own pet and one question. Observations, photos, lessons and full weeks of reports earn grants, and your rank climbs from laboratory assistant to academician, with an engraved medal for each.

## Good to know

**Is it a vet app?**

No, it's a diary. It notices worrying patterns and tells you when to see a vet, but it doesn't diagnose or treat.

**I have a rabbit, a ferret or a gecko. Will it work?**

Yes. Besides cats and dogs there are parrots, rabbits and other rodents, ferrets and reptiles, each with their own breeds, care and behaviour atlas.

**It's the first version. What if something breaks?**

Please tell me: Settings → StillCraft has buttons to report a bug or suggest an idea through the StillCraft bot.

## Get it

- **Requires:** Android 9 (API 28) or newer.
- **Permissions:** no internet at all; notifications for reminders. The phone's own camera opens only when you photograph your pet. No account, no ads, no analytics.
- **License:** MIT. Fonts: Lora, Rubik and IBM Plex Mono (OFL).

1. Download the APK on the phone.
1. Open it and allow installs from this source when Android asks.
1. Register your pet: its kind, breed and name.
