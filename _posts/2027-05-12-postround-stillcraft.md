I walk past the same houses every day and know almost nothing about them. Number fifteen has a green door. The corner house has a cat on the windowsill. That's it. I wanted a game that would give those houses people, and give me a reason to walk up to their doors.

Poste Restante makes you the postman of your own neighbourhood. The streets and house numbers are real, from OpenStreetMap; the residents are made up, a few in every house. Each morning a bag of mail waits for you, and a letter can be handed over only when you stand at the house. It's free, with no ads and no accounts. This is the first version.

## What's inside

### Your district, your round

Stand by your front door, tap "My home is here", and the district is drawn around you in pixel art: roofs, courtyards, trees, the tram lines, the river, and a house number on every building that has one in OpenStreetMap. The round starts at some three hundred metres around home and grows with every letter you deliver.

The morning bag holds letters, postcards, bills, magazines, papers for the subscribers and parcels. How full it is depends on how much you've walked lately, counted by the phone's own step counter or by Health Connect. Nothing is lost if you don't finish: what's left waits for tomorrow.

### At the door

The GPS lets you hand mail over only within thirty metres of the house, and never from a tram or a car. At the door you see the house in its colour, the blue plate with its real number, the intercom and the resident in the doorway, and you hear what they say: "The electric again! I hardly ever switch it on." Postcards are open, so they read them in front of you.

You don't have to hold the phone. Tap "Start the round", put it in your pocket, and the mail is handed over at each door by itself; what people said waits for you to read later.

{% include figure.html path="blog/postround-post/shot-1.jpg" caption="At the door: what the resident says." %}

### Letters between houses

The residents write to each other, and you carry their letters back and forth. An old man recognises a woman's voice in the bakery and writes to remind her of the skating rink in 1952, where he fell at her feet (twice, she points out; the second time on purpose). A retired candidate master and a schoolboy play a game of chess by post, move by move, and the board is drawn in every letter. A grandmother sends three jars of jam to her granddaughter far away, and you're the one who takes the parcel to the post office.

When two houses write to each other, a red thread is drawn between them on the map. Sometimes the reply is written right at the door and goes straight into your bag. When a correspondence ends, the residents write to the postman, and the letter waits in the poste restante window.

There are sixty such stories, enough for a year: a Christmas tree stuck on the fourth floor, two yards' snowmen, a starling box, the last school bell, rival mushroom pickers, a stepladder that has travelled round six flats. A new one starts every few days, and the seasonal ones wait for their time of year.

{% include figure.html path="blog/postround-post/shot-2.jpg" caption="The bag, sorted by street." %}

### Letters with no address

Every few days a letter comes with no number and no street: "To the people of the yellow five-storey house by the chemist's." The clue is made from the map itself, from the colour the house is painted, how tall it is and what stands next to it. Find it, walk up and ask. If it's the wrong house, they'll wave you on: "a house like that is to the north-east, a couple of blocks away". The letters are from the old postwoman who carried this round for thirty-eight years and still remembers the parrot on the third floor.

{% include figure.html path="blog/postround-post/shot-3.jpg" caption="Found by the signs." %}

### Telegrams and stamps

Telegrams come while you walk, for a house a short way off, with a time on them. Every street of the district has its own stamp, drawn in code: water and a bridge, a tram, a little park, roofs with a cat, a lamp in the rain, an archway. It comes with your first delivery on that street, and the album slowly fills with the streets you've walked.

{% include figure.html path="blog/postround-post/shot-4.jpg" caption="A stamp for every street." %}

### The map

Tags hang over the houses with mail, and the houses themselves glow; a telegram's house glows red. Today's route is dotted from you through the houses in order, and mail beyond the screen shows as arrows at the edge with the distance. A "?" button explains every icon.

## Good to know

**Do I have to walk to particular places?**

No. The bag is made from houses around your home. Deliver on the way to the shop or to work.

**Does it work in my town?**

Anywhere OpenStreetMap has house numbers, which is most towns.

**It's the first version. What if something breaks?**

Please tell me: Settings has buttons to report a bug or suggest an idea through the StillCraft bot.

## Get it

- **Requires:** Android 9 (API 28) or newer.
- **Permissions:** location, physical activity (the step counter), notifications, internet for the map (OpenStreetMap via OpenFreeMap) and the weather (MET Norway). Health Connect only if you choose it. Location and steps stay on the phone.
- **License:** MIT. Font: m6x11 by Daniel Linssen.

1. Download the APK on the phone.
1. Open it and allow installs from this source when Android asks.
1. Allow location, stand by your front door and tap "My home is here".
