I have killed more houseplants than I'd like to admit. Never from neglect, always from love: a watering every Sunday, whether the pot needed it or not. The plant that finally survived was the one I forgot about for a month in a north window.

So I made the app I needed then. Rootlings is a pocket field guide in the style of the handheld games I grew up with. Every plant on your windowsill has a spirit, a little pixel creature in a pot. You catch them all, and they live by the real thing: the real sun and clouds over your town, the real new leaves, the real pests. There's no watering calendar at all. Free, no ads, no account.

## What's inside

### Windows under the real sky

First you add your windows. Press the back of the phone to the glass and the compass tells you which way it looks. Then say what's outside: open sky, trees across the street, a wall close by. From then on the app works out the light on every sill, hour by hour, from where the sun really is and how much direct and diffuse light reaches the ground in your town that day (from Open-Meteo, or from MET Norway's clouds). A south sill in November gets a fraction of June's light, and a plant a metre from the glass gets less than half of what the sill gets. The panes show the real clouds, rain, snow and night.

### Catching

You catch a plant with the phone's own camera, and its spirit takes the green of your leaves from the photo, so a pale variegated pothos and a dark one look different. You pick the species from a catalogue of 133 houseplants. If yours isn't there, you describe it yourself: what it looks like, its type, how much light and water it likes, whether it hates dry air or rests in winter, the colour of its flowers.

{% include figure.html path="blog/rootlings-post/shot-2.jpg" caption="Catching: the system camera, then the species." %}

### Soil, not a calendar

This is the part I care about most. Each pot dries at its own pace, set by:

- its species;
- the light it got that day;
- the dry air of the heating season (the app notices when it has been colder than +8° outside for five days in a row).

When it's probably time, the spirit doesn't ask for water. It asks you to put a finger in the soil. You answer dry, damp or wet, and every answer teaches the estimate how fast your pots really dry. Cacti sleep through winter and ask for almost nothing until spring.

{% include figure.html path="blog/rootlings-post/shot-1.jpg" caption="A spirit's page: today's light, the soil, its forms." %}

### Forms

Every spirit has three forms. You count new leaves (or pads, or fronds) and once a month take a "molt" photo from the same spot, so you see before and after side by side. When the plant has really grown and enough time has passed, the spirit evolves, with the flicker you remember.

### Battles

Webs under the leaves, sticky spots, tiny flies over the soil? You pick what you see, and the app names one of 12 real troubles: spider mites, scale, thrips, mealybugs, fungus gnats, aphids, whitefly, root rot, sunburn, mildew, too little light, springtails. Then a battle begins. The moves are the real treatment: isolate, shower, spray, and repeat in a week, because the eggs hatch. The repeat moves unlock on their real days. "Stripe takes a warm SHOWER! It's super effective!"

{% include figure.html path="blog/rootlings-post/shot-3.jpg" caption="A wild WEBBER appeared." %}

### Nursery

A cutting in a glass of water is an egg. When the roots show, a spirit hatches, and it carries its ancestry: "child of Whiskers ← grandma Zoya's spider plant". You can keep it, or give the rooted cutting to a friend together with a short text code. The friend pastes it into their app, and the spirit arrives with its family tree.

{% include figure.html path="blog/rootlings-post/shot-4.jpg" caption="The nursery: an egg in a glass." %}

### The dex

The dex shows your species in colour, the ones you've only seen at a friend's or in a café in sepia, and the rest as shadows. It also keeps the pests you've beaten. Plants that died aren't erased: they stay in the herbarium as pressed pages, with their names, their forms and the days they lived. And when you go away, the app writes a note for whoever waters: who needs water, and roughly when.

## Good to know

**Is the care advice reliable?**

It's general advice for the common houseplants, written carefully and on the cautious side. Every home is different, which is why the app trusts your finger over its own estimate.

**What leaves the phone?**

Only the town search and the weather request: the coordinates of the town you picked go to Open-Meteo (or MET Norway). Plants, photos and notes stay on the phone.

## Get it

- **Requires:** Android 9.0 (API 28) or newer. No Google services needed.
- **Permissions:** internet (only for the weather and the town search) and notifications (the morning round).
- **License:** MIT

1. Download the APK on your phone.
1. Open it and allow installs from this source when Android asks.
1. Add the window your plants stand at, and catch the first one.
