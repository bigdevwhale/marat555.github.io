Focus timers with a pet usually work the same way: the pet is happy while you don't touch the phone, and that's about it. I wanted the pet to actually live somewhere, and the most natural place for it is the phone itself. If something lives in there, it would feel the battery, see through the camera, and wander off into the gaps between the boards when nobody is looking.

Rustle is a little creature that lives inside your phone. Turn the phone face down, and it goes out exploring and comes back with finds. It's free, with no ads and no account. This is the first version.

## What's inside

### A home inside the phone

The battery is the stove. While the phone charges, a fire burns and the kettle steams; when the charge runs out, the stove goes cold and Rustle wraps up in a blanket. In the dark it lights a candle, in bright sun it squints. Shake the phone and it gets dizzy; let it overheat and it fans itself. When storage fills up, dust gathers in the corner.

The camera is a round window onto the street. Behind it are the roofs across the way and the real sky: sunsets follow the sun where you are, and if you turn weather on, the rain, snow, fog and storms are the ones outside your own window.

{% include figure.html path="blog/rustle-post/shot-2.jpg" caption="Evening in the nook, snow over the roofs." %}

### Outings, face down

Put the phone face down and Rustle goes out: to the charging port, where the draught comes in; down to the battery cellar, warm and smelling of raisins; over the ribbon-cable bridge; into the speaker gorge, where every ring echoes for an hour; up to the observatory under the camera glass. Each new place needs a tool from the workshop: a lantern, a rope, earmuffs, a ladder.

The longer the phone lies there, the further it goes and the more it brings. A quick look at the phone is fine. Scroll for a long time and Rustle turns back halfway, with whatever it has found so far. While it's out, the outing shows in a notification.

{% include figure.html path="blog/rustle-post/shot-1.jpg" caption="Five places, opened one by one." %}

### Nights by the stove

At night, put the phone on charge and tuck Rustle in. If the screen stayed dark till morning, the night went well, and it brings back something from its dreams. In the morning there's a small present waiting.

### Finds, the workshop and the kitchen

There are 62 finds: buttons, screws, LEDs, feathers, beads, coins, bits of glass, and raisins, rusks and honey to eat. The rarest ones, the wonders, turn up only on long outings.

In the workshop the finds become tools, furniture and clothes. What counts is the shape: any thread will do as thread. The furniture moves into the nook — a matchbox bed, an armchair, a clock with a pendulum, a bottle-cap aquarium — and clothes can be tried on right there: hats, a scarf, a bow tie, glasses, a monocle, a backpack, a cape. In the kitchen the pantry turns into tea, porridge, a raisin pie and honey cake.

{% include figure.html path="blog/rustle-post/shot-3.jpg" caption="The workshop: what you can make from what it found." %}

### Yours

There are eight kinds of creature, each with its own ears, tail, wings or spines. The fur comes in eight colours, or in a colour taken from any photo: your cat, a favourite jumper, the sea. Character grows from how you live together: after ten hours of outings a tuft sticks up, seven good nights give it rosy cheeks, restless nights grow owl tufts on its ears.

{% include figure.html path="blog/rustle-post/shot-4.jpg" caption="Kind, fur and character." %}

### Why come back

Mood and hunger change outings: a cheerful Rustle brings one find more, a glum or hungry one brings one fewer. Every Monday it asks for three things — feathers for a pillow, two walks to the cellar, a good night's sleep — and gives something back for each. There are 67 achievements, ranks from tenant to house spirit, a run of days you looked after it, a tilt maze on the circuit board, and a widget for the home screen. Dark and light themes come with five background colours.

{% include figure.html path="blog/rustle-post/shot-5.jpg" caption="What mood and hunger change." %}

All the pixel art — the nook, the creatures, the finds, the icons — is drawn in code, with no image files.

## Good to know

**Does it drain the battery?**

No. During an outing the app only notes when the screen goes on and off. The light sensor and the accelerometer work only while the app is open.

**What if I need the phone during an outing?**

Use it. A quick look is fine; only long screen time sends Rustle home early.

**It's the first version. What if something breaks?**

Please tell me: Settings has buttons to report a bug or suggest an idea through the StillCraft bot.

## Get it

- **Requires:** Android 9 (API 28) or newer.
- **Permissions:** notifications for outings, optional. Internet and approximate location only for the weather in the window, and only if you turn it on.
- **License:** MIT. Font: m6x11 by Daniel Linssen.

1. Download the APK on the phone.
1. Open it and allow installs from this source when Android asks.
1. Name your creature, pick its kind and fur, then turn the phone face down for the first outing.
