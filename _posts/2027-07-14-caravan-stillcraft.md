We already had walking games on the shelf – Fogwalk and Roamling – and people who mostly get around by car or bus kept asking for something of their own. So here is a game for the rides you take anyway. In Caravan your city becomes a magic bazaar: each real district is a quarter with its own market – moon honey in the gardens, amber by the river, dwarf cogs in the forges. Buy a good in one quarter, carry it to another on your way to work, and sell it dearer.

It's free, with no ads and no account. This is the first version.

## What's inside

### Real quarters, real reasons to choose

The quarters come from OpenStreetMap, and what a quarter trades depends on what is actually in it: parks, water, old streets, universities, factories, shops, stations. A quarter pays well for the goods it wants and for goods carried from far across the city; sell the same thing in one place again and again and the price sags. Fish and bread go off, glass can break in a traffic jam, and a quarter's page shows what your pack would fetch there today.

{% include figure.html path="blog/caravan-post/shot-1.jpg" caption="The market: what each bale fetches here and what you make on it." %}

### Nothing to tap while driving

This mattered most to me. While the car moves the screen shows one calm card – the quarter and the kilometres – and the market opens only when you stop. Mark goods for the steward and he sells them for you on the way, wherever they pay most. On a bus, tram or the underground you're a passenger, so you can trade on the move. When the phone connects to your car, a «The caravan is ready» notification comes, and when the engine goes off the ride ends by itself.

### A trading house to build

Gold goes into your own trading house: ten buildings on a plot, from a crested gate and a garden to the hall, a pigeon tower and the treasury. Each one shows on the plot and helps your trade – more room in the pack, dearer sales, a daily gift from the garden, a night market after evening rides.

{% include figure.html path="blog/caravan-post/shot-2.jpg" caption="The trading house, half built." %}

### Merchants, events and curios

Eight merchants stand at the gates of their quarters – a gardener, a boatman, a keeper of keys, a stargazer, a blacksmith, a silk merchant, a caravan leader and a baker – each with four requests and a little story, and each request says plainly what to buy and where to take it. Every week the city has an event (a drought in the gardens, a festival of fire in the forges, a caravan from the south), every day brings guild orders and rumours, and sixty curios turn up on rides: on the underground, at night, in the rain, on long roads. After a ride you sometimes meet someone on the road – a pedlar who wants to swap, a dice player, a fortune-teller.

## Good to know

**Is it safe while driving?**

Yes. While the car moves there's nothing to tap; everything else waits until you stop.

**I only ride from home to work. Is that enough?**

Yes – that road becomes your trade route and you can build on it. New quarters pay more, though, so now and then another way pays off.

**What goes online?**

Map tiles, OpenStreetMap data about the quarters and the weather at the end of a ride – by coordinates, with nothing about you. Rides and progress stay on the phone, and location is read only while the app is open or a ride is on.

## Get it

- **Requires:** Android 9 (API 28) or newer.
- **Permissions:** location while the app is open or a ride is on, notifications, Bluetooth (only to recognise your car, if you choose it). No account, no ads, no analytics.
- **License:** MIT. Fonts: Nunito and Philosopher (OFL). Map © OpenStreetMap contributors.

1. Download the APK on the phone.
1. Open it and allow installs from this source when Android asks.
1. Allow location – the app finds your quarter and the ones around it.
