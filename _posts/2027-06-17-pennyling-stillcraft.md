Most of us have tried to track money and stopped after a week: the categories get boring, the numbers start to feel like a telling-off, and one day you just don't open the app. Pennyling turns the month into something you live through rather than audit. The month from payday to payday is a winter in a little burrow under an old oak. Your budget is the pantry, every day gets its ration of acorns, and snow piles up outside the window as the month runs out.

It's free, with no ads and no account, and it never connects to your bank. The only thing that leaves the phone is your town's coordinates, so the round window can show the real sky. This is the first version.

## What's inside

### Today's ration

The home screen is the burrow itself, with today's ration under it: what's left of the budget divided by the days to payday, with money for rent and subscriptions kept aside first. Five acorns melt away as you spend. A bar shows the whole month – how much has gone and how far into the month you are – and a strip of seven days shows the week against the ration. A day with no spending at all can be marked as a snow day.

### Pets that keep the books

Each spending category has a keeper: a squirrel for groceries, a raccoon for cafés, a beaver for the home, a mole for transport, a bat for subscriptions. A keeper hatches from the first few records in its category and grows with every one after – from records, not from saving, so logging a splurge is never a punishment. Ten more pets turn up for good habits: the jay for snow days, the otter for answering «was it worth it?», the fox for putting a purchase off, the badger for debt payments. A team of three lives in the burrow and doubles the crumbs for its own thing; crumbs buy furniture and surprise eggs at the fair.

### Jars, the attic, the cellar

Goals fill up as jars of jam, and a full jar holds an egg. A wish to buy something can go up to the attic to cool for a month – if you still want it then, buy it with a clear conscience; if not, the sum goes to «not spent». Subscriptions and regular payments live in the cellar, log themselves on their day and are reviewed every three months; the magpie spots spends that repeat like a subscription. Debts are boulders at the door with snowball and avalanche plans, and the beaver's dam shows how many months your savings would last.

{% include figure.html path="blog/pennyling-post/shot-2.jpg" caption="The cellar: subscriptions a year and a review of each." %}

### Was it worth it?

A week after a purchase the otter asks one question: was it worth the money? Over time the answers add up to «joy per coin» – which categories make you happy and which ones you regret. And if you'd rather not type at all, the crow can turn your bank's notifications into ready records, from the apps you mark as banks only.

### The chronicle

Statistics come in four tabs. The overview has the marmot's forecast – will the pantry last to payday at this pace – and month by month. Categories shows where money goes, with limits and personal inflation. Habits has a spending calendar, weekdays and hours, little things that add up, favourite places, the biggest spends and the cost in hours of work. Wealth has subscriptions a year, net worth and a «what if» calculator. At the end of the year there is a year in the burrow.

## Good to know

**Do I have to log every coffee?**

No. Log what you can: pets grow on records, not on perfection. Regular payments log themselves, and the crow can pick up card purchases from bank notifications.

**Does it connect to my bank?**

No logins and no bank access. The crow only reads, on the phone, the notifications of the apps you mark as banks.

**It's the first version. What if something breaks?**

Please tell me: Settings → StillCraft has buttons to report a bug or suggest an idea through the StillCraft bot.

## Get it

- **Requires:** Android 9 (API 28) or newer.
- **Permissions:** internet only for the weather in the window; notifications for the evening question; notification access only if you turn the crow on. No account, no ads, no analytics.
- **License:** MIT. Fonts: Nunito and Inter (OFL). Weather by Open-Meteo and MET Norway.

1. Download the APK on the phone.
1. Open it and allow installs from this source when Android asks.
1. Pick your currency, payday and the month's budget.
