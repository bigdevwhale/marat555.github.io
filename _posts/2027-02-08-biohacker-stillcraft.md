I've lost count of the sleep tips I've read. Morning light, no screens after nine, magnesium, a cold bedroom, mouth tape. Some of them are backed by good studies, some by one podcast, and even the good ones only tell you what happened on average to people who aren't you. I didn't want another list. I wanted a way to try one thing at a time, honestly, and find out whether it does anything for me.

Biohacker draws your body as a blueprint, chalk lines on cyanotype blue, with eight systems, and turns 1103 evidence-graded protocols into mods. You put a mod on trial for 14 days and take five quick readings a day. Then the lab compares the days with it against the days without, and you decide whether it goes into your firmware. Free, no ads, no account, no internet.

## What's inside

### The blueprint

Your body is a technical drawing with eight systems: Clock (sleep, light, rhythm), Engine (movement and strength), Furnace (food and water), Armor (hardening and prevention), Processor (focus and learning), Cooling (stress and breath), Battery (mood and meaning) and Network (people and nature). Every mod that works for you lights up as a node in its system.

### 1103 protocols

Each card says exactly what to do, why it works, when, for how long and how often, with a caution where it matters. And each one carries an honest evidence class: **A** strong, **B** good, **C** plausible, **D** weak but harmless. You can search and filter by system, evidence, time and effort, and cards come in three rarities: common, rare and legendary.

{% include figure.html path="blog/biohacker-post/shot-2.jpg" caption="A legendary card: how, why, a caution, and evidence class B." %}

### A trial, n = 1

Put a mod on trial and tick it off on the days you do it. After 14 days the lab compares your gauges on days with the mod (and the morning after) against days without it, including the two weeks before you started. Then it stamps the protocol: works, no effect, worse, or not enough data. You can install it, reject it, give it another week, or set it aside. The data hints; the call is yours.

{% include figure.html path="blog/biohacker-post/shot-1.jpg" caption="Dimming the house two hours before bed: sleep up 0.8, stamped “works”." %}

### Five readings a day

Sleep, energy, mood, focus, calm. One tap each, about ten seconds. You get one reminder a day, and only if today's readings aren't in yet.

### Power

You can't start everything at once. Trials running together can't outweigh your power: a light mod takes one unit, a heavy one three, and every two installed mods add a unit. It's a game rule, but it's also how habits stick: a few at a time.

### A parcel every day

Every day a sealed parcel arrives from the lab with a card for a protocol you haven't seen yet, leaning towards the systems you care about. Sometimes it's a broken mod: one of 96 popular hacks that didn't hold up, like detox juices or eight glasses of water a day, with what the evidence actually says.

{% include figure.html path="blog/biohacker-post/shot-3.jpg" caption="The card index: 1103 protocols and 96 broken mods to collect." %}

### Version and patch notes

You start at v1.0, and every installed mod bumps the version. Each Monday brings patch notes: what was installed and rejected, which gauge went up, known issues. Forty hidden combos, such as morning light with cold water or a walk with a friend, are found by installing both halves and drawn as gold links on the blueprint.

### Glitches, not failures

A day in the red isn't a failure; it's a glitch, and it brings an emergency patch: three short, well-backed mods for whatever sagged. Uptime counts your days of readings, and one missed day a week is bridged. Scheduled service reminds you of the check-ups that catch faults early: blood pressure, the dentist, blood tests, a mole check, screenings, vaccines, eyes, the first-aid kit.

## Good to know

**Is this medical advice?**

No. Each card summarises research and grades how strong it is, with cautions where they matter. If you have a condition, are pregnant or take medication, check with your doctor first.

**Can two weeks of my own readings prove anything?**

Not like a clinical trial, and the app says so: with too few days it stamps "not enough data". But it's a far better answer to "does this work for me?" than a hunch.
