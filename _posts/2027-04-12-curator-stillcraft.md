My phone told me the memory was full, and I opened the gallery to make room. Eleven thousand photos. Screenshots of things I no longer remember, ten takes of the same plate, pocket shots of pure black. I deleted forty and gave up. Swipe-to-delete apps didn't help either: a stack of eleven thousand cards is still eleven thousand cards, and one wrong flick and something is gone for good.

So Curator does it the way a museum would. The gallery is a storeroom, every month is a crate, and you open one crate at a time. It's free, with no ads and no internet access at all.

## What's inside

### A crate at a time

The main screen lists the crates of arrivals, one per month, each with four of its own photos on the lid and a line showing how much is left. A crate is twenty-odd cards, a couple of minutes. Special crates cut across the months: screenshots, videos (heaviest first), pictures saved from chats, everything over 20 MB.

### One gesture per photo

Every photo comes on an inventory card with its number, date, folder and size. Up writes it off, right keeps it, down keeps it and puts it into an album, left puts it aside for later, and a long press makes it an exhibit. The same four actions are buttons below the card, and undo takes the last card back.

{% include figure.html path="blog/curator-post/shot-1.jpg" caption="One photo, one gesture." %}

### The expert goes first

Before the first card, the expert has already looked at the crate: how sharp each photo is, how dark, whether it's a screenshot, and whether two shots are the same shot twice. The rejects come as one sheet. Confirm them with one button, or tap the ones you'd rather keep. It all happens on the phone, on small grey copies of the photos.

Near-identical shots taken within a minute come as one card: you pick the original, and the copies go into the act.

{% include figure.html path="blog/curator-post/shot-2.jpg" caption="Rejects, put aside before you start." %}

### A training crate

The first time, the app hands you a training crate of six stock photos and asks for one gesture at a time: swipe a blurred shot up, keep a good one, put a pug into an album, set aside the one you can't decide on, hold the best for the exhibition, and pick the sharpest frame of a burst. A wrong swipe just shakes the card.

{% include figure.html path="blog/curator-post/shot-3.jpg" caption="Every gesture, tried once." %}

### Nothing goes without a signature

A swipe never deletes anything. Written-off photos wait in the act of write-off, where you can look at each again or keep it after all. When you sign, Android asks once for the whole act, and only then is the space freed.

{% include figure.html path="blog/curator-post/shot-4.jpg" caption="Form No. 7." %}

### Albums that are real

Halls are your albums. A hall can be moved into its own folder, and from then on the phone's own gallery shows it as an album too. This week's exhibits hang on a poster with a title you can change and send.

{% include figure.html path="blog/curator-post/shot-5.jpg" caption="Halls and the exhibition of the week." %}

### The museum grows

The building on the main screen stands in scaffolding. The space you free opens the wing at 250 MB, then the gallery, the rotunda, the observatory and the sculpture garden, each with a ribbon to cut. Ranks come with the photos you've sorted, from attendant to museum director, and now and then a photo taken on this very day years ago comes out of the storeroom.

{% include figure.html path="blog/curator-post/shot-6.jpg" caption="Cut the ribbon." %}

## Good to know

**Can I delete something by accident?**

No. Photos only leave when you sign the act and confirm Android's question.

**What does the app send anywhere?**

Nothing. It has no internet permission.

**Does it have a dark theme?**

Yes: daylight gallery or the same halls at night, or follow the system.

## Get it

- **Requires:** Android 11 (API 30) or newer.
- **Permissions:** photos and videos; notifications, for an optional morning note. No internet.

1. Open it and allow installs from this source when Android asks.
