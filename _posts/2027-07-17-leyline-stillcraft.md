Caravan turned city rides into trade. Leyline takes the same rides somewhere more magical. Under the streets of your city run hidden lines of power, and every ride – by car, bus, tram, underground or bike – lights a path along them and fills your bag with whatever the way offered: mist from the river, resin from the park, copper from the market, moonlight after midnight. Back home in a cosy tower you brew potions from it, and nobody hands you the recipes.

It's free, with no ads and no account. This is the first version.

## What's inside

### Ingredients from the real road

There are eighteen of them, and each comes from something that actually happened on the way. A fast road brings wind, traffic jams bring stone, rain and snow bring storm and frost, a ride after dark brings moonlight, twilight brings dawn, the underground brings ore, and places you've never been bring star shards. The rest depends on what lay along the route – a river, a park, old streets, a university, a market, a station – read from OpenStreetMap once the ride is over.

### Experiments in the cauldron

This is the part I like most. Drop two or three different ingredients into the cauldron: guess the set and its recipe opens; miss, and the cauldron tells you whether you were warm or cold. There are twenty-four recipes – elixirs for the next rides, awakening potions for your beasts, wards, gifts for the spirits and three wonders.

{% include figure.html path="blog/leyline-post/shot-1.jpg" caption="The cauldron and the shelf of ingredients." %}

### A beast for every kind of ride

A road dragon for the car, a lantern beetle for the bus, a spark serpent for the tram, a burrow wyrm for the underground and a two-tailed fox for the bike. They grow with the kilometres, from baby to ancient, each step opened by a potion you brew, and how you ride gives them traits: night eye for late rides, rain scales for wet ones, a stone heart for patience in jams.

{% include figure.html path="blog/leyline-post/shot-2.jpg" caption="A grown road dragon and its traits." %}

### The map of power

The city is cut into 250-metre cells. The route you ride most grows from a thin trail into a great line, and where three strong paths meet a knot forms; the underground shows as dashes below ground. Places where rides keep starting and ending – home, work, a friend's – awaken into sanctums after three visits, and you can set a ward on them.

{% include figure.html path="blog/leyline-post/shot-3.jpg" caption="Lines and knots of a few weeks of rides." %}

### Spirits of the quarters

The districts are real, and each has a kind: gardens, riverside, old town, forges, market. Each has its spirit – an otter by the river, a candle owl in the old lanes, a trader magpie at the market – with four requests that say plainly what to do. Finish them and the spirit becomes your friend and adds its ingredient to every ride through its quarters. Add the omen of the day, a blooming quarter each week, the full moon, and the odd surprise on the road, and no two days are quite the same.

{% include figure.html path="blog/leyline-post/shot-4.jpg" caption="The otter of the riverside, already a friend." %}

### Nothing to tap while driving

While the car moves, the screen shows only what matters, and everything the road brought waits for you when you park. When the phone connects to your car, a notification offers to start the ride; for the bus and the underground there's a quick-settings tile.

## Good to know

**Is it safe while driving?**

Yes. While the car moves there's nothing to tap; everything waits until you stop.

**I only ride from home to work. Is that enough?**

Yes – that road grows into a great line, and home and work become sanctums. New places bring star shards, though, so now and then another way pays off.

**What goes online?**

Map tiles, OpenStreetMap data about the quarters and the route, and the weather at the end of a ride – by coordinates, with nothing about you. Rides and progress stay on the phone, and location is read only while the app is open or a ride is on.

## Get it

- **Requires:** Android 9 (API 28) or newer.
- **Permissions:** location while the app is open or a ride is on, notifications, Bluetooth (only to recognise your car, if you choose it). No account, no ads, no analytics.
- **License:** MIT. Fonts: Cormorant Garamond and Alegreya Sans (OFL). Map © OpenStreetMap contributors.

1. Download the APK on the phone.
1. Open it and allow installs from this source when Android asks.
1. Allow location – the app finds your quarter and the ones around it.
